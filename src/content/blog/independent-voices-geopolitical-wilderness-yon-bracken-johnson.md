# Independent Voices in the Geopolitical Wilderness: Michael Yon, Matt Bracken, Larry Johnson, and Al Johnson

In an era where mainstream media narratives dominate geopolitical discourse, a handful of independent voices have carved out alternative spaces for analysis and reporting. Figures like Michael Yon, Matt Bracken, Larry C. Johnson, and Al Johnson each bring distinct backgrounds and perspectives to the table. This post examines their work, their worldviews, and the broader questions their rise raises about independent geopolitical commentary.

---

## Michael Yon: The War Correspondent Who Went Rogue

Michael Yon (born 1964) is perhaps the most credentialed of the group. A former US Army Special Forces soldier turned independent war correspondent, Yon has spent more time embedded with combat units in Iraq than any other journalist, according to a 2008 New York Times profile.

His reporting from Mosul with the 1st Battalion, 24th Infantry Regiment (Deuce Four) made him a household name in military blogging circles. His photograph "Little Girl" — capturing the aftermath of a suicide bombing — was voted Time magazine's Top Photo of 2005. His book *Moment of Truth in Iraq* (2008) hit Amazon's Top 10 bestsellers.

But Yon has always been controversial. He engaged in combat alongside soldiers in Mosul, grabbing a rifle to defend wounded troops when others froze. He feuded publicly with generals — McChrystal, Menard — accusing them of incompetence. The Los Angeles Times labelled him a "lone gun." He was deported from Hong Kong in 2020 after reporting on the pro-democracy protests.

In recent years, Yon has shifted focus to the **Darién Gap** — the treacherous jungle corridor between Colombia and Panama used by migrants heading to the United States. Since 2021, he has reported extensively from the ground, documenting what he describes as an orchestrated migration crisis. His work in the Darién Gap has drawn both praise for on-the-ground reporting and criticism for the company he keeps, including collaborations with figures like Alex Jones and Laura Loomer.

### Key Themes in Yon's Work:
- **First-hand observation over press-release journalism**: Yon has argued that embedded journalism, for all its flaws, produces more accurate reporting than hotel-based bureau journalism.
- **Information warfare**: He views many geopolitical narratives as deliberate psychological operations.
- **Migration as weaponised policy**: His Darién Gap reporting frames mass migration as a coordinated operation enabled by NGOs and government policy.

## Matt Bracken: From Navy SEAL to Dystopian Prophet

Matthew J. Bracken (born 1957) brings a different pedigree. A former US Navy SEAL who served on SEAL Teams 2 and 4, including deployment to Beirut in 1983, Bracken transitioned from military service to boatbuilding, sailing, and eventually writing.

His *Enemies* trilogy — *Enemies Foreign and Domestic* (2003), *Domestic Enemies: The Reconquista* (2006), and *Foreign Enemies and Traitors* (2009) — depicts a United States descending into civil conflict through government tyranny, mass migration, and institutional betrayal. The first novel, which opens with a false-flag terrorist attack at a football stadium used to justify gun confiscation, briefly became the most downloaded free Kindle book on Amazon in 2012.

Bracken's fiction reads as predictive fiction or what critics call "fictionalised conspiracy theories." His work has been influential in militia and Patriot movement circles. His essay "Tet, Take Two: Islam's 2016 European Offensive" — comparing the 2015 European migrant crisis to the Vietnam War's Tet Offensive — went viral and was endorsed by Oath Keepers founder Stewart Rhodes.

Bracken has been a frequent guest on Alex Jones' InfoWars, and his December 2020 appearance — urging Americans to march on Washington and "if necessary storm right into the Capitol" — was cited in the January 6th Committee Report.

### Key Themes in Bracken's Work:
- **Civilisational collapse**: His novels depict a progressive unravelling of American institutions leading to armed conflict.
- **Counter-jihad analysis**: His essay work focuses on what he perceives as an Islamic infiltration of Western societies.
- **Second Amendment absolutism**: Government gun confiscation is the central trigger in his fictional universe.
- **Preparedness and resistance**: His protagonists are typically prepared individuals who resist federal overreach.

## Larry C. Johnson: The Ex-CIA Analyst Who Crossed the Line

Larry C. Johnson is a former CIA analyst and State Department counterterrorism official who has become one of the most prominent pro-Russia voices in Western independent media. After four years at the CIA and a stint at the State Department's Office of Counterterrorism, Johnson moved to the private sector in 1993.

Johnson first gained public attention as a terrorism commentator in the late 1990s and early 2000s — notably writing a July 2001 New York Times op-ed titled "The Declining Terrorist Threat" just two months before 9/11. He later became a fierce critic of the Bush administration over the Iraq War and the Valerie Plame affair.

Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Johnson has become a fixture on pro-Kremlin media. Voice of America reported that he has been cited hundreds of times in Russian state media, including Izvestia, Sputnik, and RIA Novosti. In March 2025, he was one of three media personalities invited to interview Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov in Moscow — a signal of his standing in Kremlin-aligned information circles.

Johnson runs the blog **Sonar21** (sonar21.com) and co-founded Veteran Intelligence Professionals for Sanity (VIPS), a group that has challenged official US intelligence narratives. He was also the source behind the debunked 2017 claim that British intelligence agency GCHQ wiretapped Donald Trump's campaign on Obama's orders.

### Key Themes in Johnson's Work:
- **Pro-Russia geopolitical analysis**: Johnson argues that Western media fundamentally misrepresents the Ukraine conflict and Russian strategic objectives.
- **Intelligence community critique**: Having worked inside the system, he leverages his credentials to challenge official intelligence assessments.
- **Media narrative deconstruction**: His commentary focuses on exposing what he sees as propaganda and disinformation from Western governments and mainstream media.

## Al Johnson: The Researcher in the Shadows

Less publicly prominent than Yon, Bracken, or Larry Johnson, Albert "Al" Johnson operates in the realm of independent research and analysis. While Yon reports from conflict zones, Bracken writes bestselling novels, and Larry Johnson commands a large following as a former intelligence official, Al Johnson represents the quieter analytical side of independent geopolitical inquiry — connecting dots between disparate events, policy decisions, and institutional behaviour.

## Barnem World: The Documentary Lens

The independent geopolitical commentary ecosystem extends beyond individual commentators into documentary filmmaking. Projects like "Barnem World" represent a growing trend of alternative media that seeks to visualise and dramatise the themes these analysts discuss — migration crises, information warfare, institutional decay — through the cinematic medium. These films serve as cultural artefacts of the broader independent geopolitical movement, translating analytical arguments into narrative form for wider audiences.

## The Convergence: What These Voices Share

Despite their different approaches — journalism, fiction, intelligence analysis, research, film — these figures converge on several themes:

### 1. Distrust of Institutional Narratives
All operate from a fundamental scepticism toward government, media, and academic institutions.

### 2. The Migration Crisis as Central Geopolitical Event
Yon's Darién Gap reporting, Bracken's "Tet Take Two" essay, and related analysis all treat mass migration not as a humanitarian issue but as a geopolitical weapon.

### 3. Information Warfare as the Primary Battleground
All see the contest over narratives as the decisive theatre of modern conflict.

### 4. Individual Agency Over Institutional Authority
Whether it's Yon picking up a rifle in combat, Bracken's protagonists building parallel systems, Larry Johnson leveraging his CIA credentials to challenge the agency, or independent researchers crowdsourcing analysis outside academia, all privilege individual initiative over institutional credentialism.

## Critical Perspectives

Any honest examination requires acknowledging significant criticism:

- **Michael Yon** has been accused of anti-Semitic rhetoric regarding NGO funding of migration. His comfort women revisionism has drawn condemnation from historians.
- **Matt Bracken** has been classified by the Southern Poverty Law Center as associated with the Patriot movement. His role in January 6th rhetoric has been widely criticised.
- **Larry C. Johnson** has been documented by Voice of America as amplifying pro-Kremlin disinformation. He spread the debunked Michelle Obama "whitey tape" hoax in 2008 and the false GCHQ wiretapping claim in 2017.
- The broader ecosystem these figures inhabit raises questions about the relationship between independent journalism, political activism, and extremism.

---

## Why These Voices Matter

Regardless of where one stands on their conclusions, these figures matter because they represent a growing constituency that has lost faith in institutional sources of information. Understanding their methods, motivations, blind spots, and occasional insights is essential for comprehending the full spectrum of geopolitical discourse in the 2020s.

The responsible path lies in engagement with critical thinking, verification of claims, and honest assessment of what institutional journalism sometimes fails to provide.
