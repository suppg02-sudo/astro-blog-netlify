With Discord announcing mandatory government photo ID verification for age verification, many users are looking for alternatives. This breakdown evaluates the best Discord alternatives across four key categories: voice calling, text channels, community, and usability.

## Voice Calling

**Steam Chat** offers sufficient bitrate for casual conversations with group voice calls and channels. Its main drawback is slightly higher latency than Discord.

**Mumble**, an OG option, provides the best audio quality with positional audio for gaming, but requires manual server setup — you need someone willing to be a server admin.

**TeamSpeak** offers a more polished experience similar to Discord but requires a paid license for servers hosting more than 31 users.

## Text Channels

**Matrix** stands out as a self-hostable, open-source backend with end-to-end encryption. Users connect via clients like Element, FluffyChat, or Cinny. The variety of clients is both a strength and a weakness — choice paralysis can set in.

**Sto** (formerly Revolt) shows promise with community-run servers and secure private messaging, though it is in early stages and has a small user base.

**Slack**, despite its corporate reputation, offers excellent organized chat with threaded conversations on its free tier. Its paid plan is actually cheaper than Discord Nitro — ironic when you consider how reluctant Nitro subscribers would be to pay for Slack.

## Community

TeamSpeak currently has the most dedicated communities among the alternatives. However, all alternatives struggle with the network effect — without mass user adoption, none can match Discord's community momentum. Apps like Sto, Matrix, and Circle have potential but need more users to become viable community hubs.

## Usability

**Fluxer** is essentially a Discord clone — open source and self-hostable — but lacks mobile support. **Matrix's** experience varies by client; Element is straightforward, while Comet looks similar to Discord. **Steam Chat**, when treated as a standard messaging app rather than a Discord replacement, becomes intuitive with excellent gaming integration for inviting and joining games.

## The Verdict

Steam Chat emerges as the best all-in-one replacement for most users, offering great voice, chat, and gaming integration. The biggest universal challenge is community migration — getting friends to switch platforms.
