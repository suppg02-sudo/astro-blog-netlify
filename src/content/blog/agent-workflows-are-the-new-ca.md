> **TL;DR**: Box CEO Aaron Levie and top VCs are betting big on agentic AI workflows. The real opportunity isn't building agents — it's the human work of structuring data, mapping processes, and designing the governance layer that makes agents useful. Early-career professionals who master this will command outsized influence.

## Quick Summary

- **Aaron Levie** (Box CEO) says there's a "huge opportunity" for people to reimagine workflows for a world of agents
- **$2.8 billion** invested in agentic AI startups in H1 2025 alone
- **53% of all global VC funding** in Q1 2025 went to AI — $59.6 billion
- The work isn't coding agents — it's **structuring unstructured data, mapping processes, and building governance**
- Early-career professionals are uniquely positioned to become "AI Gurus" inside enterprises

## The Gap Nobody Talks About

Everyone's excited about AI agents that can autonomously execute tasks. But Aaron Levie drew a sharp distinction that most coverage misses: coding assistants like GitHub Copilot or Claude Code work out of the box because developers already speak the language. For the rest of knowledge work — legal review, M&A due diligence, contract management, clinical research — there's no such shortcut.

> "There's really no way to shortcut any of this work. It has to be done by a person or people on the team."

This is the gap. Someone has to:
- **Structure unstructured data** so agents can access it
- **Map existing processes** end-to-end
- **Build skills and execution plans** for agents
- **Connect disparate systems** into coherent workflows
- **Design human oversight** — where validation happens, how exceptions get handled

This isn't a weekend project. It's the new operational layer of every enterprise.

## The Career Play

Alex Lieberman (@businessbarista) put it bluntly: "You could be the most junior person in your company, but if you're truly viewed as the AI Guru, you are wildly valuable and have way more leverage than you think."

His examples are concrete:

| Role | Agentic Workflow Opportunity |
|------|------------------------------|
| SDR | Map inbound process, build agent to automate qualification |
| Engineer | Lead internal enablement on Claude Code |
| Growth Marketer | Build Reddit-mining paid media pipeline at scale |

The pattern is consistent: **understand the workflow deeply, then connect it to the tooling**. Seniority doesn't matter. Workflow literacy does.

## Where The Capital Is Flowing

The venture capital thesis is clear:

- **Glean** raised $150M at $7.2B valuation for enterprise AI agents surfacing knowledge across tools
- **Cohere** closed $500M to scale enterprise agentic AI behind corporate firewalls
- **Sovereign wealth funds** participated in $46 billion of AI venture transactions in the first 9 months of 2025
- VC question has shifted from "does it recommend?" to **"does it close the loop from input to execution?"**

Jake Flomenberg of Wing Venture Capital: *"The companies growing fastest are the ones that identified a workflow or security gap created by GenAI adoption, then executed relentlessly on product-market fit."*

## The Platform Shift

Levie expects **100 to 1,000 times more agents than people** operating inside enterprise systems. That ratio breaks the per-seat SaaS pricing model that's existed for two decades. It also creates an entirely new category of human work: the person who designs, validates, and governs the agent layer.

Box's own product, [Box Automate](https://www.box.com/automate), embodies this — breaking workflows into segments where each can be augmented with AI or kept deterministic based on risk tolerance. The design explicitly creates "demarcation points" where agents operate within bounded, auditable scope.

## Why This Matters Now

Levie calls this "day one of agent adoption in the enterprise." 2025 was the year of pilots. What comes next is rapid scaling. The people who know how to build what agents need — structured data, mapped processes, governance frameworks — will be scarce before they become abundant.

The opportunity window is open. It won't stay open forever.
