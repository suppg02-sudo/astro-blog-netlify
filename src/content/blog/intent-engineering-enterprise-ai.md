# Intent Engineering: The Critical Missing Layer in Enterprise AI

February 24, 2026

## The Problem AI Solved Too Well

In January 2025, Klarna reported its AI customer service agent handled work equivalent to 853 full-time employees, saving the company $60 million. In the same earnings cycle, the CEO admitted the AI strategy had "cost something far more valuable than $60 million"—and he was still trying to buy it back.

This isn't another "AI is overhyped" story. It's the opposite: **the AI worked too well**.

The distinction between AI that fails and AI that succeeds at the wrong thing is the most important unsolved problem in enterprise AI today.

## Introducing Intent Engineering

**Intent engineering** is the discipline of making organizational purpose—goals, values, trade-offs, decision boundaries—machine-readable and machine-actionable. When you deploy autonomous systems, they need to optimize for what your company actually needs, not just what it can measure.

Klarna's AI agent knew how to resolve tickets fast. It didn't know that the real organizational intent was to build lasting customer relationships that drive lifetime value in a competitive fintech market. Those are profoundly different goals requiring profoundly different decisions.

## Three Disciplines of AI

| Discipline | Era | What It Addresses |
|------------|-----|-------------------|
| **Prompt Engineering** | 2022-2024 | How do I talk to AI? |
| **Context Engineering** | 2024-2026 | What does AI need to know? |
| **Intent Engineering** | 2026+ | What does AI need to WANT? |

Context engineering tells agents what to know. Intent engineering tells agents what to want.

## The Three-Layer Intent Gap

### Layer 1: Unified Context Infrastructure

Every team currently rolls their own context stack. One team pipes Slack through custom RAG, another exports Google Docs to a vector store, a third builds an MCP server for Salesforce but not Jira. This is the "shadow agents" problem—and it mirrors the shadow IT crisis with higher stakes because agents don't just access data, they act on it.

### Layer 2: Coherent AI Worker Toolkit

Employees use fragmented AI workflows: Claude for research, ChatGPT for drafting, Cursor for code, Perplexity for fact-checking. None of these workflows are transferable, measurable, or improvable by anyone else.

There's a massive difference between **AI activity** (30% gains from bolting AI onto workflows) and **AI fluency** (300% gains from rethinking workflows around AI capabilities).

### Layer 3: Intent Engineering Proper

This layer almost certainly doesn't exist in your business. OKRs were designed for people—they assume human judgment about prioritization, trade-offs, and exceptions. Agents don't absorb company culture through osmosis during 6 months of all-hands meetings and hallway conversations.

**What agents need:**

- **Goal structures** they can interpret—not "increase satisfaction" but specific signals, data sources, authorized actions, and trade-off parameters
- **Delegation frameworks** with decision boundaries (when X conflicts with Y, here's the resolution hierarchy)
- **Feedback mechanisms** that close the loop on alignment

## Microsoft Copilot: The Enterprise Cautionary Tale

Microsoft poured billions into Copilot, embedded AI into every Office application, and launched an aggressive sales campaign. 85% of Fortune 500 companies adopted it.

**Then adoption stalled hard:**

- Only 5% moved from pilot to scale
- Only 3% of Microsoft 365 users became paid Copilot users
- Microsoft slashed internal sales targets
- Employees at major companies downgraded licenses

The fundamental issue: deploying AI tools without organizational intent alignment is like hiring 40,000 employees and never telling them what the company values or how to make decisions.

## The Enterprise Reality

| Statistic | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Companies yet to see tangible AI value | 74% |
| AI pilots failing to scale | 30% |
| Companies without AI job redesign | 84% |
| Organizations with mature agent governance | 21% |

These aren't technology failures. They're **intent failures**. The models work. The context pipelines are getting better. What's missing is the organizational infrastructure connecting AI capability to organizational purpose.

## What Solutions Look Like

### Infrastructure Level

Build composable, vendor-agnostic architecture. Treat context infrastructure like your data warehouse strategy—as core strategic investment, not just an IT project.

### Workflow Level

Create an **Organizational Capability Map**: which workflows are agent-ready, which are agent-augmented with human-in-the-loop, and which remain human-only. This is a living document, not a Confluence page that gets forgotten.

### Alignment Level

Build **Goal Translation Infrastructure** that converts human-readable objectives into agent-actionable parameters: decision boundaries, escalation rules, value hierarchies, and feedback loops for alignment drift.

Emerging frameworks include Google's Agent Development Kit and DeepMind's five autonomy levels (Operator, Collaborator, Consultant, Approver, Observer).

## The 2026 AI Race

For three years, the AI race was framed as an intelligence race: who has the best model, the biggest context window, the highest benchmark scores.

That framing made sense when models were the bottleneck. But models aren't the bottleneck anymore.

**The race is an intent race.**

A company with a mediocre model and extraordinary organizational intent infrastructure will outperform a company with a frontier model and fragmented, inaccessible, unaligned knowledge—every single time.

The most important AI investment in 2026 isn't another model subscription or Copilot license. It's **organizational intent architecture**: making your company's goals, values, decision frameworks, and trade-off hierarchies discoverable, structured, and agent-actionable.
