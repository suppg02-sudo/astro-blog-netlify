# The 5-Step Framework for Running Your Business on AI

## The Reality Gap

Over the past few months, I set up Claude Code environments inside over 20 companies—law firms, agencies, property management companies, healthcare clinics, and home services. Different industries, different sizes, completely different levels of technical ability.

The gap between **using AI** and **actually running your business on it** is way bigger than most people think.

Every company looked basically the same when it came to AI: someone on the team had tried ChatGPT. Usually the founder, maybe for emails or brainstorming. They liked it. But nothing had actually changed in how the business was running day-to-day.

- Client onboarding still took 3 days with six handoffs
- Reports were still compiled by hand every week
- One property management company had **three people spending 15 hours per week** just triaging maintenance requests

For agencies, industry benchmark for billable utilization sits around 60-70%. That means 20-30% of everyone's time goes to admin and internal stuff that doesn't make money. On a 12-person team, that's effectively **three or four people's hours gone every week**.

---

## The 5-Step Framework

After 20+ implementations, here is the playbook:

### 1. MAP

Before touching any AI tooling, figure out what to actually automate. Most people pick what sounds cool instead of what hurts the most.

**How to do it:**

1. Grab a spreadsheet, write down every workflow your team does regularly
2. Score each one on three things (1-5):
   - How many hours per week does it eat?
   - How directly does it impact revenue?
   - How feasible is it to automate right now?
3. Add them up and rank them
4. Your top three are what you build first

This is called an **Automation Priority Matrix**. Takes about an hour. It prevents the most common mistake: building some fancy automation that saves 20 minutes a month while the team bleeds 15 hours a week on something that should have been first.

**Top three almost always fall into:**
- **Intake and onboarding** (what happens when a new client/lead comes in)
- **Reporting and data** (stuff your team manually compiles on schedule)
- **Communication** (follow-ups, status updates, notifications)

---

### 2. FOUNDATION

This is the step most people skip—and why their AI gives generic outputs.

**The biggest mistake:** Jumping straight into building automations without giving AI context about your business. It's like hiring someone and never onboarding them.

**Three pieces to the foundation:**

1. **CLAUDE.md file** — Lives in the root of your Claude Code project. Tells Claude everything about the company. Don't just put your company description—include:
   - How you name files
   - Your tech stack
   - How you want things formatted
   - How your team communicates with clients
   - Your brand voice
   - What Claude should NEVER do in your workspace
   
   The more specific and opinionated you are, the less time you spend correcting output.

2. **Memory** — Claude Code can persist memory across all sessions. Decisions made last week/month, files worked on, preferences set—all carry forward. If you're starting from scratch every time, you don't have a system. You have a chatbot with extra steps.

3. **Connect your tools** — CRM, project management, email, analytics—wherever your team lives. MCP integrations are how you do this. Takes under 10 minutes.

Once the foundation is in, AI stops being generic and starts understanding your specific business.

---

### 3. BUILD THREE

Don't build everything. Build three.

The rule: **Three automations maximum in the first phase.**

Why three?
- Forces prioritization
- Creates quick wins the team can see
- Prevents scope creep
- Builds momentum

From your Automation Priority Matrix, build only the top three. Nothing else until those are running smoothly.

---

### 4. SCALE UP

Once your three automations are running, scale them:

- Add edge case handling
- Connect more data sources
- Expand to adjacent workflows
- Train the team on how to work WITH the AI

This is where the compound effect starts.

---

### 5. COMPOUND

AI implementations compound. Each automation builds on the last:

- Data from automation #1 feeds automation #2
- Patterns learned improve automation #3
- Team trust increases, adoption accelerates
- Time saved gets reinvested in higher-value work

The businesses seeing the biggest returns aren't the ones with the fanciest AI. They're the ones who followed the process.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **Map first** — Use the Automation Priority Matrix. One hour of planning saves months of wasted effort.
2. **Build the foundation** — CLAUDE.md, memory, and tool connections. Without context, AI is generic.
3. **Limit to three** — Force prioritization, create wins, prevent scope creep.
4. **Scale deliberately** — Add complexity only after foundations work.
5. **Compound effects** — Each automation builds on the last.

---

## The Bottom Line

Everyone knows AI matters. Everyone knows it's the future. But most don't know where to start.

The ones who figure it out follow a process. They don't chase shiny tools. They map their pain points, build their foundation, execute on three things, and let the compound effect take over.

That's the playbook. Screenshot it. Use it.
