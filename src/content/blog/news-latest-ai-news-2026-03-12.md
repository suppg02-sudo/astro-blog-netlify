### AI Company Announcements & Disputes

**Anthropic Sues US Department of Defense Over "Supply Chain Risk" Label**
AI startup Anthropic has filed lawsuits against the U.S. Department of Defense (DoD) in California and Washington, D.C., challenging its designation as a "supply chain risk." The dispute stems from Anthropic's refusal to allow unrestricted military use of its AI systems, citing concerns over mass surveillance and autonomous weapons. This designation could bar government contractors from using Anthropic's models, potentially costing the company billions in lost revenue. Microsoft has filed an amicus brief supporting Anthropic, arguing the DoD's decision could disrupt suppliers and impact military access to advanced technology, while also emphasizing the need to prevent AI use for mass surveillance or autonomous warfare.

**OpenAI Acquires Promptfoo to Enhance AI Security**
OpenAI has acquired Promptfoo, an AI security firm specializing in automated vulnerability testing for AI systems. This strategic move aims to integrate automated security testing directly into OpenAI's model and infrastructure layers, allowing teams to identify vulnerabilities early and deploy secure AI from the outset. This acquisition reflects a growing industry focus on ensuring the safety and reliability of increasingly complex AI applications.

**OpenAI Faces Backlash Over Pentagon Deal**
Despite its acquisition of Promptfoo, OpenAI is experiencing internal and external criticism following an agreement with the U.S. Department of Defense to deploy its AI technology for military and security operations. The controversy intensified with the resignation of a senior robotics executive and concerns from users and rivals about the potential for advanced AI systems to be used in warfare and domestic surveillance. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman stated the company would revise the agreement to explicitly ban domestic surveillance and require additional approval for intelligence agency use.

**Google DeepMind Names New London HQ "Platform 37," Expands AI Research**
Google DeepMind has named its new London headquarters "Platform 37," a tribute to both its King's Cross railway station neighbor and "Move 37," a pivotal play by AlphaGo in its historic Go match. The facility signifies continued investment in UK-based AI research infrastructure, supporting teams working on frontier models and safety evaluation, and reinforcing talent attraction and partnerships in the local ecosystem. Google DeepMind also launched its first robotics accelerator for European startups, focusing on physical AI.
