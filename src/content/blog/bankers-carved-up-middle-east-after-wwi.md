# The Bankers Who Carved Up the Middle East After WWI

*How European powers, banking dynasties, and oil interests secretly divided a region—and why the consequences still shape our world today.*

---

## The Invisible Architecture Behind Every Border

Every border dispute in today's Middle East, every war over contested land, every refugee camp that has existed for generations—there's almost always a line you can trace backward through time. Follow that line far enough, past the revolutions and coups and failed peace processes, and you end up in the same place: the closed rooms of European imperial powers in 1916, where a handful of aristocrats, financiers, and diplomats drew lines across a map of a region most had never visited.

They drew those lines in weeks. The people living inside them are still dealing with the consequences more than a century later.

But this isn't simply a story about imperialism. What has been examined far less often—and matters just as much—is the **financial dimension**. The banking dynasties, the oil interests, the invisible architecture of capital that made these territorial decisions not just possible but *functionally inevitable*.

> Behind every border drawn on that map, there was a balance sheet that somebody was reading very carefully. Behind every promise made and broken, there was a transaction.

---

## The Ottoman Empire: "The Sick Man of Europe"

For six centuries, the Ottoman Empire controlled the lands we now call Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Palestine, and vast stretches beyond. At its height in the 17th century, it was among the most sophisticated political organisms on Earth—a sprawling multi-ethnic, multi-faith empire governing populations that spoke dozens of languages under a single administrative framework.

But by the 19th century, the empire was rotting from within:
- Centuries of administrative corruption
- Devastating military defeats against Russia and rising European powers
- Economic stagnation and failure to industrialize
- Relentless pressure from European colonialism

European diplomats mockingly called it "the sick man of Europe"—a phrase containing both contempt and barely concealed appetite. A sick man's possessions need to go somewhere when he finally dies.

## Oil: The Strategic Commodity That Changed Everything

What none of the powers circling the Ottoman territories had fully reckoned with was something buried deep beneath the soil.

### The D'Arcy Concession (1901)

William Knox D'Arcy, a British entrepreneur, negotiated an extraordinary agreement with the Shah of Persia:
- £20,000 in cash
- Equal value in company shares
- 16% of future profits
- **In exchange for**: Exclusive rights to prospect for oil across most of Persia for **60 years**

It was one of the most lopsided agreements in the history of natural resource extraction.

For years, D'Arcy's men drilled and found almost nothing. His investors grew desperate. Finally, a letter arrived telling him to pack up—the project was over.

That letter was making its way across the desert when, at 4 AM on **May 26, 1908**, a 50-foot gusher of petroleum exploded upward at Masjid Suleiman, lighting up the Persian night sky.

### Churchill's Strategic Gamble

In 1911, a young Winston Churchill became convinced that Britain's naval supremacy was at risk. The Royal Navy ran on coal, but oil-powered ships could outrun, outmaneuver, and out-range them.

The problem: Britain had almost no domestic oil supply.

Two months before WWI began, the British government purchased a **51% controlling stake** in the Anglo-Persian Oil Company. This wasn't merely a financial investment—it was a declaration of strategic intent that would shape British foreign policy for 50 years.

## Three Contradictory Promises About the Same Land

### Promise 1: To the Arabs (McMahon-Hussein Correspondence, 1915-1916)

Britain needed the Arabs to revolt against the Ottomans. The Ottoman Sultan had declared jihad, threatening to ignite the 70 million Muslims living under British rule in India.

Beginning in July 1915, Sir Henry McMahon exchanged 10 letters with Hussein bin Ali, the Sharif of Mecca. Britain promised recognition of Arab independence across:
- Most of the Arabian Peninsula
- Syria
- Palestine
- Mesopotamia

The language was deliberately vague with careful hedging—but Hussein read it as a genuine commitment. An Arab revolt in exchange for an Arab state.

Hussein launched the Arab Revolt in June 1916. His son Faisal led Arab forces, alongside T.E. Lawrence ("Lawrence of Arabia"). They captured Aqaba, sabotaged railways, and fought their way into Damascus, believing their arrival was the first step toward the independent state they had been promised.

**Thousands died for that belief. They were deceived from the very beginning.**

### Promise 2: To France (Sykes-Picot Agreement, May 1916)

At the exact same moment Arabs were mobilizing in the desert, two diplomats were constructing an arrangement that would render the promised Arab state meaningless.

- **Sir Mark Sykes** (British) and **François Georges-Picot** (French) negotiated a secret arrangement
- France would take Syria and Lebanon
- Britain would take Iraq and Palestine
- Remaining territories would become nominally independent states under European influence

Sykes famously claimed to have drawn the border "from the E in Acre to the last K in Kirkuk"—borders determining the political existence of millions of people sketched with reference to the typography of city names on a map.

The line cut through communities that had coexisted for centuries, separating tribes from grazing lands, merchants from their cities, families from their relatives. None of this was treated as a serious obstacle.

### Promise 3: To the Jews (Balfour Declaration, November 1917)

By late 1917, Britain was exhausted. Russia was in revolutionary chaos. America had entered the war but wasn't yet fully committed. The government was searching for anything that could shift the balance.

On November 2, 1917, Foreign Secretary Arthur Balfour wrote a 67-word letter to **Lord Lionel Walter Rothschild**, stating that Britain viewed with favor "the establishment in Palestine of a national home for the Jewish people."

Why address it to a Rothschild? The answer reveals the financial architecture underlying these decisions.

## The Rothschild Family's Extraordinary Role

### Baron Edmond de Rothschild's 50-Year Investment

Beginning in 1882, Baron Edmond James de Rothschild (French branch) began directing substantial personal wealth toward Jewish settlement in Ottoman Palestine. Motivated by devastating Russian pogroms, he didn't wait for political solutions.

Over 50 years, he spent an estimated **$6 million** (an enormous sum for the era) on:
- Settlements: Rishon LeZion, Petah Tikva, Zikhron Ya'akov, Rosh Pina, Metula
- Infrastructure: Houses, roads, wineries, glass factories, silk production, schools, synagogues
- Agriculture: French experts teaching modern farming, extensive irrigation systems
- Administration: Networks throughout Palestine reporting to him in exhaustive detail

Legend among settlers said "Rothschild knew when every goat gave birth in the colonies he supported."

By 1918, Rothschild-associated land holdings covered roughly **1/20th of Palestine's fertile territory**—not conquered or seized, but purchased transaction by transaction over four decades.

In 1924, he organized his investments under PICA (Palestine Jewish Colonization Association), which eventually acquired **125,000+ acres** and helped establish **40+ settlements**.

> The map of modern Israel is in a very direct and literal sense partly a map of Rothschild investment. Rothschild Boulevard in Tel Aviv is named for him. Cities like Binyamina and Givat Ada are named for his family.

### The Structural Connection

The Rothschild family held major stakes in **Royal Dutch Shell** throughout this period—making them significant financial beneficiaries of both:
1. British mandate over Palestine (protecting 50 years of investment)
2. Access to Mesopotamian oil (through oil company equity)

The same family receiving the Balfour Declaration also held shares in oil companies benefiting from the Iraq mandate. These connections were "not conspiratorial"—they were structural, the natural result of a family positioned at the intersection of capital, government, and empire.

## The Bolshevik Revelation

When the Bolsheviks seized power in November 1917, they raided the Tsarist archives and found the secret treaties. On November 23, 1917, they published the full text of Sykes-Picot in Russian newspapers. Within days, the Manchester Guardian reprinted it in English.

**The world now knew what had been arranged in secret.**

Arab leaders who had been fighting alongside British forces learned that their patrons had been planning to divide their land between France and Britain from the very beginning.

Faisal, commanding Arab forces in the field, read the text with "devastation that cannot be adequately described." He had taken the British at their word. He had led men into the desert to die for a promise that was never seriously intended to be kept.

T.E. Lawrence later wrote of himself as "a fraud"—a man who had made promises to dying men on behalf of a government with no intention of honoring them. The McMahon-Hussein correspondence was kept classified until **1939**.

## The San Remo Settlement (April 1920)

The postwar settlement formalized the arrangements:
- Britain received mandates over Palestine and Iraq
- France received Syria and Lebanon
- The Balfour Declaration was incorporated into international law

### The Mosul Deal

The most striking evidence of oil's centrality came in the Mosul question. France agreed to cede Mosul to British Iraq in exchange for a **25% share** in the oil company developing Mosul's petroleum.

The same conference, on the same day, formalized both:
1. The territorial borders of mandated Iraq
2. The oil company shareholding arrangement

**The borders of modern Iraq were drawn in significant part as the result of an oil company equity negotiation.**

---

## The Red Line Agreement (1928)

The final arrangement gave 23.75% each to Anglo-Persian, Royal Dutch Shell, Compagnie Française des Pétroles, and an American consortium. Gulbenkian retained his 5%.

All partners agreed not to pursue oil concessions independently within former Ottoman borders without offering other partners participation rights. Gulbenkian drew the boundary with a **red crayon**—hence "Red Line Agreement."

This secret cartel:
- Controlled oil development across the region for 20 years
- Suppressed Iraqi production to maintain artificial scarcity and elevated prices
- Kept ordinary people ignorant that their resources were being managed by foreign corporations

## The Consequences: Still Drawing Blood

### The Kurds
Promised an autonomous homeland in 1920, the commitment was erased in 1923 under Turkish pressure. A people of **30-40 million**—one of the largest ethnic groups on Earth—left stateless, fighting for recognition ever since.

### Iraq
Assembled from three Ottoman provinces (Mosul, Baghdad, Basra) with no shared political tradition:
- Sunni Arabs in center/west
- Shia Arabs in south
- Kurds in north

Borders drawn for British access to oil and trade routes. Faisal was "parachuted into a country he had never lived in" to govern people who hadn't asked for him. Sectarian divisions were institutionalized as a colonial governance strategy.

### Syria and Lebanon
Faisal's Arab Kingdom of Syria lasted less than two years before French forces destroyed it at the Battle of Maysalun (July 1920). France subdivided the mandate deliberately to prevent unified Arab nationalism. Lebanon's communal complexity today—which produced civil war and repeated political crises—is largely the product of French administrative decisions made in the 1920s.

### Palestine
The Arab population (90% in 1917) was never consulted. Balfour himself wrote privately in 1919 that Western powers did not intend "to go through even the form of consulting the wishes of the present inhabitants."

The Arab Revolt of 1936-1939 was brutally suppressed, decimating Palestinian leadership. When 1948 came, Palestinians were fighting from profound organizational weakness. Roughly **700,000** were displaced in the Nakba—many refugees remain in camps to this day.

## The Bottom Line

The modern Middle East was manufactured in those rooms by those men for those interests.

The suffering continuing there is not the result of ancient hatreds erupting spontaneously. It is the result of **decisions made by people who knew exactly what they were doing**, who understood that the promises they were making were contradictory, and who calculated that by the time the contradictions became impossible to manage, they would be in a strong enough position to manage them anyway.

They were wrong about the last part. But by the time that became clear, the damage was done, the map was drawn, and the people living inside it were left to figure out how to survive it.

> **"The lines drawn in 1916 are still drawing blood."**
