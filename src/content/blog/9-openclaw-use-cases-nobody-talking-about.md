## Introduction

I've been at a handful of meetings in the last two weeks with founders, CEOs, and executives talking about AI. The question on everyone's mind is: **What are you really doing with OpenClaw?**

OpenClaw, developed by Peter Steinberger in a couple of months, has become a viral sensation of 2026—a personal AI assistant or AI agent framework recently acquired by Sam Altman and OpenAI. While many people talk about controlling their Sleep Number bed or Nest thermostat, business owners are asking a different question: **How can I use this technology to grow my business?**

In this article, I'll share nine ways that I'm using OpenClaw and people I know are using OpenClaw to drive real results for real businesses. No hype fluff—just practical applications for growth, profitability, and working smarter.

## Use Case #1: Daily Research Briefing

My OpenClaw agent, named Janet, goes out and looks around the web—Twitter, Reddit, Google, news sources—for things she thinks I need to know about. This is a curation step because Janet knows basically everything about me.

I've written a lot into her "soul"—an official document that lives in my OpenClaw setup where she knows what I'm interested in, what I might want to know more about, and where I want to focus my energy for the day. She surfaces just those handful of things she knows I need to pay attention to.

### The Benefit

As opposed to doom-scrolling Reddit or Twitter or LinkedIn, your OpenClaw agent brings that stuff to you. You don't have to go onto Twitter and see all the stuff that distracts you (frankly terrible for your mental health). My OpenClaw brings things to me. I figure out if I want to dive into any of them, then move on.

### Meeting Preparation

I apply this to meetings as well. Janet has access to my calendar and email. She'll say, "Hey, you have this meeting at 11:00. This is who John is. These are things you talked about last time from my notetaker. This is what you need to be prepared for."

Daily prep for me: news, calendar, action items that she thinks I might need to pay attention to for the day ahead.

## Use Case #2: Email Triage

When you go into your inbox each morning, how many emails do you get that are just absolute garbage? If you're anything like me, it's probably half.

Janet goes in and cleans up a lot of those. There's specialized software out there like Inbox Zero that does this as well. So if you're not using OpenClaw but want this kind of benefit, check out something like inboxzero.ai for a great way to take some cognitive load off you.

My OpenClaw does this for me. It's not perfect—there'll be some things left out that I need to go through manually—but it's rare that she gets rid of stuff I actually needed to see. I'm happy for her to be a little less intrusive than to over-archive things I actually need.

## Use Case #3: AI-Powered CRM

For a long time, people have been talking about wanting a personal CRM, but the reality is the market for it would be terrible because no one would pay for this. Well, now we have it.

If you give your OpenClaw access to your email, it can build this kind of AI-powered CRM for you. If you've been in sales, the worst possible thing you can do is spend time manually updating your CRM.

### How It Works

AI is smart. It knows:

- Who this person is
- What your last contact with them was
- What action items you might need to take away
- What reminders you need

I just use Notion for my CRM as a Notion database. My OpenClaw will say, "These are the next steps that need to happen. Maybe set a reminder for me to follow up with someone in a week or two if we're talking about a YouTube sponsorship deal or something like that."

### Finding Business Leads

My OpenClaw scours my email for potential business leads that I need for my YouTube channel (sponsors, services, etc.) and brings those to me again—offloading some cognitive load so I don't have to worry about this. She worries about it and surfaces only things I need to worry about.

Creating a virtual CRM that's AI-powered, backed by data in my email and in Notion, is a huge win.

## Use Case #4: Network Expansion

Something I don't hear anyone else talking about: this kind of second-layer connection based on your existing contacts.

I have an email out to John right now. Who else out there is like John that maybe I need to connect with? That's what I'm always wondering: How can I broaden my network to other interesting, fun, and impactful people that I can offer value to or that I just want to get to know?

### How It Works

Janet has access to my email, knows who I'm talking to, and goes and finds people similar to that for me to connect with. A lot of times, this is on LinkedIn. LinkedIn, love it or hate it, is where business people hang out.

It will suggest people for me to reach out to proactively—they're doing interesting things in AI, or they run a business I might be able to help. Not just worrying about your existing group of connections and your network, but ways to smartly grow it and intentionally grow it again with use of AI.

## Use Case #5: Business Intelligence from Social Content

Bringing in context from social content: Reddit, Twitter, LinkedIn, YouTube—talking about things that are important to me in my business.

Castus is a podcast hosting SAS platform. What are people saying in the world about podcasting that I need to be aware of? How can we use that to create content, marketing collateral, positioning updates for my business?

### Continuous Monitoring

While my first tip (daily briefing) was about me personally, this one's more about my business. What kind of business intelligence is going on automatically 24/7, 365 on my Mac Mini?

I set Janet up on her own Mac Mini. What's going on out there that I need to know about for my business and that I can go actually do things from?

This is going out, getting information from the world of podcasting in my case, but it could be sheet metal manufacturing or landscaping or AI technology for you. What's going on out there that your business needs to go know about? How can you extract that information, put it into a format that you, your agents, and your team can actually go do stuff with to be more successful?

## Use Case #6: Marketing in a Box

We're going to talk about my agent swarm (agent team) in a minute, but if you only have one agent, the instructions and kind of purpose I would give them is to create a marketing sub-agent.

### Training Your Marketing Agent

You can imagine training this sub-agent on:

- You and your brand
- Your positioning
- Things going on in your world
- Who your customers are
- Channels you like to market on

Then just let it go wild. Let it test and iterate and learn from things it's doing.

### Unshackle Your Agent

I think it's a really powerful way to unshackle your agent. In my case, Janet is the main agent and then Atlas is our marketing agent. He has privileges to do a lot of things when it comes to marketing, but he's always told: **"Hey, learn, iterate, and document what you're doing so you get smarter over time."**

This is where actual implementation of your AI agent can really vary. If you're like, "Man, watching this is overwhelming. I really want to get involved," I do help some businesses set up their AI infrastructure and agents so you can get benefits of things like this without having to put in all the work of learning like I have by doing.

Marketing in a box—if you're going to go past the first agent to a sub-agent or a specialized agent, I would just say train it on marketing. This is going to be the highest leverage activity you can get.

## Use Case #7: Content Research with Skills

Last week, I was sitting there and I said, "Hey, I want Janet and I want my agents to help me with this YouTube channel." We built a little set of skills.

### What Are Skills?

Skills are specialized files in your OpenClaw setup that give personality and jobs or SOPs (Standard Operating Procedures) to your agents.

### How I Use It for YouTube

We set up a set of these SOPs or skills for my agent team to research YouTube on topics I might create videos about. Janet actually came up with this exact video topic:

- Title options
- Thumbnail prompts for Google Gemini, Nano Banana, all these kinds
- A video outline

Instead of me flipping on the camera and saying, "Gosh, what am I going to do?" It's gone out: done thinking, done work, done research to prepare me to come here and hopefully be successful.

### Apply This Concept

Take this concept and apply it to whatever you do in your business. If you're an executive and you have a lot of one-on-ones or board meetings or things like that, how can you deploy an agent with skills?

Skills are just markdown files—a text file that gives kind of an SOP or a job description. How can you set up that job description to tell your agents what you want them to do, in format and tone, and what you want to get out of this?

For me, that was YouTube. For you, that probably will be something else. This is a really specific example of how you might think about intentionally deploying an agent with skills to achieve a certain thing in your business.

This is a really meta-level concept: you're not going to go from zero to what I'm talking about here in a day. This is weeks' worth of work if you're doing this by yourself. But you do want to start small and just iterate and kind of stack things on top of each other.

## Use Case #8: Customer Support Triage

If you're like, "Wo, Craig, I would never release an AI agent to customer support," I agree with you. We definitely don't do that.

### Our Current Setup

We have a bot on our website at Castus and it answers a lot of questions. It's reduced our customer support by about 40%. But people are absolutely still required to answer a lot of questions that customers have for us.

### What Agents Can Do

There are certain things that even past a bot, an agent can do:

- Go in and pull things out of a ticket
- Create a GitHub issue
- Spin up a CodeSandbox instance to fix a bug
- Create a pull request
- Integrate with Sentry monitoring

This is more of a concept. To be honest, we have this about half-baked right now. It's surfacing things to us, but it's not doing work yet.

### Conservative Approach

I think that's a nice first step for those of us who are a little agent skeptic: "Oh, I don't want to give away the reins. I don't want to give away the keys to the kingdom or control to an agent for it to go just do a bunch of stuff."

First step: just bubbling up things that you need to be aware of so you and your team can go do things with it. Honestly, that's kind of where we are.

But I see a lot of promise here because there's a lot of repetitive manual things that we all do in a business. And that's kind of the whole point of AI: we just don't need to do those things so we can elevate and do something more impactful and interesting with our time.

## Use Case #9: Agent Teams (Swarms)

I promised I would get to this: agent teams.

### When You're Getting Started

Set up your first agent and just talk to them one-on-one. They're going to build a bunch of context, a bunch of skills. They'll have a lot of ability. But as you go, you'll start spreading that into dedicated areas of focus.

### Structure: Main Agent + Specialized Sub-Agents

The easiest way is you have one main agent (Janet in my case) and then you have specialized sub-agents that handle specific areas of your business.

### Sub-Agent Categories

This could be:

- **Content**
- **Marketing**
- **Operations**

It could be things in your personal life:

- **Fitness**
- **Finance**
- **Relationships**

You might share one agent with your spouse or your partner.

### The Concept

I think this is more of a conceptual framework: the main agent is always the router. It can create jobs or spawn sub-agents to do specific things.

Again, this is going to be different for everyone. And this is why things like OpenClaw are so powerful: they can let you do whatever you want. And that's also why they're so complicated—it's just a box and you have to kind of build things that you want from it.

## Key Takeaways

### Start Small, Iterate Gradually

You're not going to go from zero to sophisticated agent teams in a day. This is weeks' worth of work. Start with one main agent, then expand gradually.

### Marketing is Highest Leverage

If you're going to create a specialized sub-agent, make it marketing. Train it on your brand, positioning, and channels. Let it test, iterate, and learn.

### Conservative Automation Wins

Prefer under-automation to avoid mistakes. Surface insights first, then automate. Bubble up things you need to be aware of so you and your team can take action.

### OpenClaw is Flexible

OpenClaw is a framework, not a pre-packaged solution. You have to build what you want from it. This flexibility is both powerful (unlimited possibilities) and complex (requires work).

### You're Not Too Late

OpenClaw has only been around for about 3 months. Most people haven't used this yet. If you haven't done any of this yet, you're not too late. But you want to be aware of what's going on here.

### AI Agents Are the Future

At a high level, what's going on is AI agents are coming into mainstream, and they're absolutely the future of how we're going to interact with technology and the internet. While you don't need a sophisticated OpenClaw setup today, you will need to be thinking about how you and your business can adopt AI agents into the future.

## Conclusion

These nine use cases demonstrate how OpenClaw and AI agents can drive real business results: faster growth, increased profitability, and working smarter.
