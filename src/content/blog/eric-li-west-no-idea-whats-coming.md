In a recent wide-ranging conversation, venture capitalist Eric Li presents a compelling analysis of why the Western world fundamentally misunderstands China's trajectory. Li argues that while both China and Western countries benefited from globalization, their outcomes have diverged dramatically.

## The Paradox of Globalization

Li opens with a striking observation: "It's an amazing thing if you compare China and America for instance both countries benefited tremendously from globalization." Yet while China's GDP grew 10x and median income increased eightfold, America's median income has been dropping, stagnant, and declining despite massive GDP growth.

**Where has the money gone?** Li points to yellow vest protests in Paris and widespread dissatisfaction in Germany as evidence that Western elites have lost touch with their populations.

## Capitalism vs. Socialism: A Fundamental Difference

The core of Li's argument centers on how China successfully engaged globalization while rejecting Western "globalism" — the ideological framework of liberal democracy and market capitalism.

**In China**, capital exists but is subordinate to political authority. As Li explains:

> "In China, in a socialist country, we have tremendous capital, great entrepreneurs, lots of wealth, but capital is beneath political authority. Our national political authority will never allow capital to determine the future of our country."

**In America**, by contrast, the 100 wealthiest people have outsized influence over the political establishment.

Li makes the distinction starkly clear: The difference isn't about the absence of capital — it's about **who controls capital**.

## China's Unique Success

Li contends that China is the **only country** that has successfully navigated globalization on its own terms:

1. **Economic Achievement**: Second largest economy by nominal terms, largest by PPP, world's largest trading nation (115-120 countries count China as their largest trading partner vs. 63 for US)
2. **Poverty Reduction**: Lifted 800 million people out of poverty, with 100 million more in the last 10 years alone
3. **Infrastructure Development**: Belt and Road Initiative building physical and digital connectivity
4. **Anti-Corruption**: Most intense anti-corruption movement in 2,000 years, dramatically improving trust in institutions

## The Western Dilemma

Li presents a striking analysis that should concern Western policymakers:

> "The leading power, the incumbent leading power is dismantling the system it built by itself for its benefit. It wants to ditch it."

This is historically unprecedented — never before has a leading power deliberately deconstructed the institutions it created. Europe built its systems assuming the US-led order would continue forever, leaving it "in trouble" as that foundation crumbles.

## China's Alternative Proposition

Rather than imposing ideological values, China proposes a "globalization of diversity" or pluralism through initiatives like:

- **16+1 Framework**: Economic integration without ideological imposition
- **Belt and Road**: Infrastructure-led development respecting national sovereignty
- **Common But Differentiated Responsibility**: Climate policy acknowledging cumulative historical emissions

As Li articulates it: "A globalization that respects national sovereignty, respects differences, a globalization of diversity."

Li quotes Xi Jinping's analogy: "It's like a pair of shoes — whether or not they fit, only you could tell when you walk." Countries need space to develop their own paths.

## The Era of Hard Power

The Western rules-based international order is collapsing, Li argues, giving way to an era of "warring states" where:

- Soft power failed to deliver
- Rules-based systems didn't benefit ordinary people
- Hard power competition is intensifying
- Europe is particularly vulnerable, having not prepared for this reality

Europe's "illusion" of a peaceful rules-based order where everyone operates under shared rules has evaporated.

## Assessment of Xi Jinping's Leadership

Li provides a candid "interim assessment" of Xi Jinping's 10-year tenure on the three crises that threatened to end China's experiment in 2012:

### 1. Corruption
**Prediction**: Failing to handle corruption would end the party
**Reality**: Most intense anti-corruption campaign in 2,000 years
**Outcome**: Trust in government institutions has risen while US trust has plummeted

### 2. Economic Inequality
**Prediction**: Widening wealth gap would trigger revolution
**Reality**: Gini coefficient is improving through targeted poverty reduction
**Outcome**: Nearly 100 million lifted from absolute poverty (hardest segment), though a long way remains

### 3. Environmental Degradation
**Prediction**: Would cause social unrest
**Reality**: Blue skies most days in Beijing; China becoming global leader in green technologies
**Outcome**: Environmental improvements while maintaining economic growth

## China's Next Frontier: Moving From Middle Income Trap

Li identifies China's critical challenge: avoiding the middle-income trap through two directions:

1. **Increasing Industrial Capacity Value**: Moving from 12% gross margin to 35% (worth $5-10 trillion annually)
2. **Developing New Industries**: Alternative energy, new materials, bioengineering, life sciences

"Market forces could not be relied upon to engineer this transition," Li argues. "This had to be done through political initiatives top down."

## Key Takeaways

Eric Li's analysis suggests that the Western world's anxiety about China may stem from a fundamental misunderstanding:

- **China is not trying to export socialism** but offering a different model of globalization
- **The problem isn't China's system** but the West's failure to adapt to a changing world
- **Institutions built by the West** are being dismantled by the West itself
- **Europe faces the hardest position** having invested in a rules-based order that no longer exists

The West, Li argues, needs to entertain China's proposition: interconnectivity and globalization while respecting cultural and civilizational integrity and national sovereignty.

> "People need dignity. People need community. People need a sense of belonging. People need cultural integrity. Countries need to be able to develop in their own ways."
