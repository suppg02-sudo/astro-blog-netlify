## The Energy-Population Connection

Moriarty draws a direct line between energy production and human population that most analysts miss entirely. Overlaying a chart of energy production from 1865 (the birth of the oil industry) against population growth reveals identical curves. The implication is stark: **energy is population**. Reduce energy production by 20% permanently, and population follows — not immediately, but inevitably.

This isn't theoretical. We're already seeing the early effects:

- Fresh produce prices in the UK have **doubled to tripled** — a basket of tomatoes that cost £6 a week ago now costs £20
- Service stations in Darwin and New South Wales, Australia have **completely run out of diesel**
- Natural gas in Europe has **doubled**
- Diesel is up approximately 50%
- The GCC countries face food supply disruption as Red Sea and Strait of Hormuz shipping routes are compromised

## The Hormuz Strait and the Saudi Pipeline

The geopolitical situation has escalated dramatically. Saudi Arabia has two sea routes for oil: the Strait of Hormuz on the east coast and the Red Sea on the west coast, connected by an internal pipeline capable of moving 6.5 million barrels per day. When Iran blockaded the Strait of Hormuz (with insurance companies refusing to insure cargo), Saudi Arabia started pumping through the pipeline to the Red Sea.

Then the Houthis declared: "We're on the side of Iran. You want to ship oil through here? You've got a problem."

If another 6-12 million barrels per day come off the market, Moriarty says "catastrophe doesn't even begin to describe it."

## Iran: 40 Years of Preparation

Moriarty, a Vietnam veteran (1968-1970), offers a military perspective that cuts through the political rhetoric:

> "Iran has prepared for 40 years. They understood they were going to be attacked. Israel made it clear for 40 years they intended to attack Iran."

Key points about Iran's position:

- Iran has a **distributed energy system** — bombing substations in Tehran might shut power for a day, but can't take down the country
- Iran gets only **2% of its water** from desalination, compared to Israel's 80% and the GCC's 50-70%
- Iran could shut down the global economy in a week by taking out desalination plants in the GCC and Israel
- The country is **twice the size of Texas** with 92 million people and the most mountainous terrain in the region
- They have the **most sophisticated missile and ballistic systems** in the Middle East
- No one has ever conquered Persia in 3,000 years of warfare

Moriarty draws parallels to Vietnam: "In Vietnam, North Vietnam had 20 million people. Iran has 92 million. North Vietnam was a small country. Iran is twice the size of Texas. North Vietnam had under-equipped soldiers. Iran has the most sophisticated missile and ballistic systems."

## The Private Credit Time Bomb

Perhaps the most alarming part of the conversation centers on private credit markets. Moriarty describes it as a hand grenade with the pin pulled and the spoon released.

The mechanism: Over the past 20 years, lending shifted from traditional banks to private credit companies. These firms borrowed from banks, then issued high-interest loans to risky borrowers with little chance of repayment. When the Japanese carry trade started blowing up, investors in these private credit companies demanded their money back — and the firms couldn't meet the cash demands.

> "Somebody pulled the pin on a hand grenade. What happens when you pull the pin and let the spoon go? We're about to learn."

This sits on top of:
- The Japanese bond market blowing up
- A $7 trillion Japanese carry trade unwinding
- $2 trillion in cryptocurrency wiped out
- A liquidity crisis visible in the repo market

## Gold, Silver, and the Margin Call

Counterintuitively, gold and silver have sold off during this crisis. Moriarty explains this as a **margin call phenomenon**:

> "When the margin clerk calls, what do you sell? The most liquid asset."

GCC countries, facing existential disruption, are dumping gold and silver for cash. This is not a fundamental bearish signal — it's forced liquidation. Moriarty placed orders the day of the interview, viewing the crash as "the most wonderful thing in the world." He points out that silver at 7% down has absolutely no fundamental justification.

He also made a major bet on the Brent ETF two weeks prior, calling it "probably the easiest trade I've ever seen" — if oil went from $3 to $12 in 1973 during a smaller crisis, where does it go in a crisis three times larger?

## The Collapse of the American Empire

Moriarty invokes Ray Dalio's framework of societal collapse through six stages. He believes the Munich Security Conference — where Rubio declared "there are no rules, we're the biggest and best" — marked entry into stage six. After stage six, collapse happens "far more suddenly than you can imagine."

The parallels to the Soviet Union collapse in 1991 are instructive but worrying. Russians endured shortages from 1920 to 1991 — they were prepared. Americans are not. With 400 million guns among 330 million people, the potential for civil unrest is enormous.

> "We're going to see the biggest transition in American history from being the richest country in the world to the not richest country in the world."
