Machine learning tools are no longer reserved for enterprise giants with deep pockets. Today's ML-powered platforms are increasingly accessible to small and midsize businesses, offering new ways to automate repetitive work, sharpen insights and help lean teams punch above their weight. But while it's easier than ever to deploy automation, it's much harder to ensure those early investments won't hold a company back as it scales.

If they aren't built with change in mind, systems that work fine for a startup can become rigid, fragmented or expensive as the company grows. Below, members of [Forbes Technology Council](https://councils.forbes.com/forbestechcouncil) share practical steps small-business leaders can take to ensure their ML-powered tools remain flexible, relevant and valuable over the long haul.

## Choose Flexible, Short-Term Contracts

As orthogonal as it sounds, monthly contracts may be the way to go to prevent annualized lock-in. Given how fast-moving AI tools are, this option provides the flexibility to move to something better—for example, replacing Cursor with Claude Code in less than a year, given how far Claude has evolved in terms of its coding abilities. — Ryan Donovan, Thinkific

## Build A Strong Data Foundation With Modular MLOps

Smaller businesses can take one smart, small step by investing early in a strong data foundation combined with a lightweight, modular MLOps setup. AI systems are mainly about data, so organizing data in a clean, secure and well-structured way and making it easily accessible through standardized pipelines is foundational. — Malik Alyousef, Mozn

## Automate Around Outcomes, Not Tasks

A smart first step is to focus on automation that's built around outcomes, not just tasks. Early ML tools should make it easy to understand why something happened, not just automate an action. As businesses grow, clarity and adaptability matter more than speed. Systems that preserve context and can evolve with complexity stay useful long after the initial rollout. — Huzaifa Sial, Careforce

## Prioritize Stable, Modular Systems

The smartest step is to invest in stable, well-designed systems that treat models as interchangeable components. Clean organizational context plus the agility around vendor changes means you're first in line to reap all the benefits of a space that evolves so quickly. Just as importantly, these systems should be tolerant of the stochastic nature of ML by designing for variability. — Viswajit Nair, Antim Labs

## Stabilize And Document Your Data

Stabilize the data. Early ML works because the system is small and everyone still "knows" the data. Growth breaks that fast. New hires, new tools, new markets—suddenly the tribal knowledge that held everything together is gone. The smartest move is to document where your data comes from, what it means and who owns it. Clean, well-understood data is what lets automation evolve with your business. — Ginger Dhaliwal, Upflex

## Anchor ML Tools To Clear Workflows And Owners

From experience, it is better to anchor ML tools to a specific workflow—such as automating customer support triage or sales lead scoring—with a clear owner, rather than deploying generic AI features. Tools that plug directly into systems like CRM, ERP or ticketing platforms scale far better than standalone pilots that teams bypass over time. — Nishanth Billa, Santander Corporate & Investment Banking (Santander CIB)

## Choose Tools That Solve Today's Problems And Can Evolve

Focus on ML tools that solve specific operational problems but are flexible enough to evolve. That means choosing solutions that use consistent data definitions, support and integrations and that keep people involved in decision-making. Automation that improves visibility or reduces manual work now can scale effectively if it's designed to integrate with flexible platforms as the business grows. — Mark Wallin, Phillips Connect

## Seek To Enhance People's Work, Not Replace It

Smaller businesses should focus on ML tools that look to enhance people's work, not replace it. Choosing solutions that integrate with existing workflows allows intelligent automation to grow alongside your business. This starts with leaders staying close to how tools are being used day to day so they can make smarter, longer-term investments that benefit their broader teams. — Jo Debecker, Akkodis

## Design For Modularity And Portability

Design for modularity and data portability from day one. Use tools with open APIs, clean data schemas and clear ownership of your data. This makes it easy to swap models, add automation or scale without redoing everything as the business grows. — Diwakar Dwivedi, Circular Edge

## Own Your Data And Measurement Layer

For smaller businesses, a smart move is to own your data and measurement layer, not just the tool. Start logging inputs and outputs, human overrides, and a few business KPIs tied to each automation—time saved, error rate, customer impact—in your system of record. That way, as you grow, you can retrain, upgrade or even swap vendors without losing the context that makes automation valuable. — Vivek Acharya

## Don't Lock Today's Assumptions Into The Model

One smart step is to avoid locking today's assumptions into the model. Select ML tools that allow business intent and priorities to be adjusted over time without requiring retraining. That separation between learning and control is what keeps automation useful as the business evolves. — Ben Gutkovich, Superlinked

## Decouple Your Workflows From ML Implementations

Build ML automation around stable interfaces, not specific models. By decoupling workflows from ML implementations using APIs, data contracts and monitoring, small businesses can retrain, replace or scale models without reworking core processes. This keeps automation flexible, auditable and valuable as the organization grows. — Karan Kumar Ratra, Walmart Global Tech

## Standardize Workflows And Data First

Don't automate chaos. First, standardize the workflow and data the tool depends on. Automation only scales what already exists. If the process is clear and repeatable, ML grows with you. If it isn't, it just locks in the mess. — Oleg Sadikov, DeviQA

## Plan For Ongoing ML QA

Plan for ongoing quality assurance activities to validate ML results. This is not a "set it and forget it" tool because when the models are faced with new scenarios, they may get it wrong. Budgeting and planning for a human in the loop is critical to ensure the models meet business objectives for the long haul. — Amy Brown, Authenticx

## Prioritize Tools With Full Data Portability

Tools that work perfectly for a business with 10 employees may not serve 100 employees, so one smart step is ensuring complete data ownership and portability from the start. This means prioritizing tools that allow full data export and don't create proprietary data formats that lock a business into specific vendors. — Kevin Cushnie, MC Systems

## Leverage A Trusted MSP

An SMB should leverage a managed service provider that is invested in turning its IT operations into a future-ready platform that provides value to the business. Risk management, quality, speed and efficiency are what businesses are looking for, but automation efforts often fall flat because a business lacks skills and time to manage the new-age tech and complexities day-to-day operating brings. — Nicholas Nelson, Integris

## Separate Decision Logic From Prediction Models

Separate the decision logic from the prediction model as early as possible. Use ML to recommend how to act on predictions, but ensure that business rules, thresholds and override settings remain configurable by users. As you grow, your models will evolve. By reducing expensive model rewrites and mitigating risk, you will keep your automation strategies aligned with evolving business strategies. — Kavitha Thiyagarajan, Ascendion

## Invest In People Who Understand Model Predictions

Invest in people who understand why models predict what they do, not just how to deploy them. The first ML tool you buy will be obsolete before your business doubles in size. What survives isn't technology; it's the judgment to know when to override automation. — Joseph Byrum, Consilience AI

## Keep Core Logic In-House

Retain your differentiated business logic and your "secret sauce" for your customer models and leverage the cloud for everything else. That will allow you to scale and make changes effectively as the business grows. — Amirtha Raman, Amazon

## Be Wary Of Tool Sprawl

Whether your business is small or large, the principles for leveraging ML tools remain the same; the difference is scale. A key risk is unchecked tool sprawl. Start by applying ML to peripheral, low-risk tasks; understanding the implications; and building toward a sustainable system rather than consuming complexity all at once. — Hari Sonnenahalli, NTT Data Business Solutions
