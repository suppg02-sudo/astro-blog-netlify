# Greenhouse Growing Guide: Maximize Your Spring 2026 Harvest

*Get months ahead of outdoor growers with these proven greenhouse strategies*

A greenhouse transforms what's possible in the UK climate. While outdoor growers wait for the last frost, you're already harvesting. This guide covers what to grow, when to start, and how to manage your greenhouse for maximum productivity.

---

## 🎯 What a Greenhouse Actually Buys You

| Benefit | Time Gained | Real Impact |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| **Earlier sowings** | 4-6 weeks | Tomatoes from April, not May |
| **Extended season** | 4-8 weeks | Harvest into November |
| **Tender crops** | Impossible outdoors | Aubergines, melons, chillies |
| **Reliable ripening** | Crucial for UK | Tomatoes that actually turn red |
| **Weather protection** | No rain damage | Consistent quality |
| **Pest barrier** | Partial protection | Fewer slug/snail issues |

## 🍅 The Tomato Strategy

Tomatoes are the #1 greenhouse crop. Here's how to do them right:

### Variety Selection

| Type | Varieties | Best For |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Cordon (indeterminate)** | Gardener's Delight, Shirley, Sungold | Greenhouse, trained up canes |
| **Bush (determinate)** | Red Alert, Tumbler | Hanging baskets, containers |
| **Beefsteak** | Marmande, Big Boy | Greenhouse only, need warmth |
| **Cherry** | Sungold, Sweet Million | Reliable croppers |

### The Timeline

| Week of Year | Task | Notes |
|--------------|------|-------|
| **Week 12 (now)** | Sow seeds | 18-21°C, one per pot |
| **Week 14** | Pot on | Move to 12cm pots |
| **Week 16** | Harden off | Start ventilating greenhouse |
| **Week 18** | Plant out | Into greenhouse border or grow bags |
| **Week 20** | First flowers | Start feeding |
| **Week 24** | First fruits | Support heavy trusses |
| **Week 28** | First harvest | Early July |

### Critical Care Points

1. **Side shoot removal** - Pinch out shoots between leaf and main stem (cordon varieties only)
2. **Regular watering** - Consistent moisture prevents blossom end rot
3. **Feeding** - Tomorite or similar once first truss sets
4. **Ventilation** - Open doors/vents on warm days to prevent fungal issues
5. **Support** - Tie in regularly as plants grow

## 🌶 Chillies and Peppers: The Heat Seekers

These need more warmth than tomatoes and take longer to ripen. Patience is key.

### Heat Requirements

| Stage | Temperature | Duration |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| **Germination** | 25-28°C | 7-14 days |
| **Seedling** | 18-21°C | 6-8 weeks |
| **Growing on** | 21-24°C day / 16°C night | Ongoing |
| **Fruiting** | 24°C+ | Needs sustained heat |

### Hot Tips

- **Chillies need stress to be hot** - Slightly underwatering increases capsaicin
- **Feed sparingly** - Too much nitrogen = lots of leaves, few fruits
- **Hand pollinate** - Shake plants or use a brush when flowers appear
- **Pick green or wait for red** - Green chillies are edible, red are sweeter
- **Overwinter plants** - Cut back and keep frost-free for head start next year

### Recommended Varieties

| Heat Level | Variety | Days to Harvest |
|------------|---------|-----------------|
| **Mild** | Padron, Anaheim | 70-80 |
| **Medium** | Jalapeño, Cayenne | 75-85 |
| **Hot** | Habanero, Scotch Bonnet | 90-100 |
| **Super hot** | Ghost, Reaper | 100-120 |

## 🥒 Cucumbers: The Thirsty Growers

Greenhouse cucumbers are different from outdoor varieties - choose parthenocarpic (all-female, no pollination needed) types.

### Why Greenhouse Cucumbers?

- 3-4x the yield of outdoor plants
- No bitterness from stress
- Smoother skins
- Longer harvest season

### Key Care

1. **Consistent watering** - Never let them dry out (bitter fruits)
2. **High humidity** - They love it moist
3. **Regular feeding** - Weekly with tomato feed
4. **Support** - Train up strings or canes
5. **Remove male flowers** - On non-parthenocarpic varieties (bitter fruits if pollinated)

### Recommended Varieties

| Variety | Type | Notes |
|---------|------|-------|
| **Carmen** | All-female | RHS AGM, reliable |
| **Passandra** | All-female | Mini cucumbers |
| **La Diva** | Parthenocarpic | Good for beginners |
| **Marketmore** | Standard | Outdoor or greenhouse |

## 🍆 Aubergines (Eggplant): The Challenge

These are the trickiest common greenhouse crop. They need sustained heat and resent cold nights.

### Critical Success Factors

| Factor | Requirement | Consequence of Failure |
|--------|-------------|------------------------|
| **Temperature** | 20°C+ day, 16°C+ night | Poor growth, no flowers |
| **Pollination** | Hand pollinate or shake | Flowers drop without fruit |
| **Feeding** | Weekly when flowering | Small fruits |
| **Support** | Tie in heavy fruits | Stems break |

### Recommended Varieties

- **Black Beauty** - Classic, reliable
- **Fairy Tale** - Mini aubergines, prolific
- **Rosa Bianca** - Italian heirloom, beautiful

### Realistic Expectations

In a cool summer, expect 3-5 fruits per plant. In a good summer, 8-12. They're worth the effort but require commitment.

## 🌿 Herbs: The Easy Wins

Herbs thrive in greenhouses and give quick returns.

### What Grows Well

| Herb | Sow Method | First Harvest |
|------|------------|---------------|
| **Basil** | Seed, multiple batches | 6-8 weeks |
| **Coriander** | Seed, succession sow | 4-6 weeks |
| **Parsley** | Seed (slow to germinate) | 10-12 weeks |
| **Dill** | Direct sow | 6-8 weeks |
| **Chives** | Seed or division | 8-10 weeks |

### Pro Tips

- **Basil hates cold** - Never below 10°C
- **Coriander bolts** - Sow little and often, keep cool
- **Mint in pots** - Never in greenhouse borders (invasive)
- **Sage, rosemary, thyme** - Better outdoors or in containers

## 🌡️ Greenhouse Management: The Essentials

### Ventilation

**The #1 mistake UK greenhouse growers make is not ventilating enough.**

| Temperature | Action |
|-------------|--------|
| **Below 15°C** | Keep closed |
| **15-20°C** | Open roof vents slightly |
| **20-25°C** | Open roof and side vents |
| **Above 25°C** | Open everything, consider shade |
| **Above 30°C** | Damp down floor, use fan |

**Rule of thumb:** On any day above 10°C, open vents by mid-morning. Close before temperatures drop in late afternoon.

### Watering

| Month | Frequency | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| **March-April** | Every 2-3 days | Check daily |
| **May-June** | Daily | Morning or evening |
| **July-August** | Daily, twice on hot days | Never let plants wilt |
| **September** | Every 1-2 days | Reduce as light drops |
| **October** | Every 2-3 days | Most crops finishing |

### Shading

**When to shade:** Mid-May to mid-August

**Options:**
- **Shade paint** - Apply to outside, washes off
- **Shade netting** - Clip to inside of glass
- **Bamboo blinds** - Roll down on hot days

**Don't over-shade** - Tomatoes need full sun for flavour.

### Heating (Optional)

If you want year-round growing or to protect tender plants:

| Option | Cost | Best For |
|--------|------|----------|
| **Paraffin heater** | Low | Frost protection only |
| **Electric fan heater** | Medium | Precise temperature control |
| **Bubble wrap** | Very low | Insulation, not heating |
| **Hot bed** | Medium | Early seed starting |

## 📅 Your 8-Week Action Plan

### Week 1 (Now): Foundation
- [ ] Clean greenhouse thoroughly (disinfect to remove pests/diseases)
- [ ] Check vents and door work properly
- [ ] Order/install automatic vent openers
- [ ] Sow tomatoes, peppers, chillies, aubergines
- [ ] Set up propagator or warm space for germination
- [ ] Check heating system if you have one

### Week 2: Preparation
- [ ] Prepare greenhouse borders or set up grow bags
- [ ] Install support strings/canes for tomatoes
- [ ] Set up watering system (hose, cans, irrigation)
- [ ] Order shade netting or paint
- [ ] Sow cucumbers, melons if space allows
- [ ] Pot on any seedlings that are ready

### Week 3: Hardening
- [ ] Start ventilating on warm days
- [ ] Move germinated seedlings to greenhouse (protect at night)
- [ ] Sow basil and other herbs
- [ ] Check for pests (aphids, whitefly)
- [ ] Install sticky traps for monitoring

### Week 4: Growth
- [ ] Increase ventilation as temperatures rise
- [ ] Start feeding seedlings with weak fertilizer
- [ ] Sow second batch of basil/coriander
- [ ] Check supports are secure for climbing plants
- [ ] Water more frequently

### Week 5: Transition
- [ ] Plant out tomatoes, peppers into final positions
- [ ] Apply mulch to retain moisture
- [ ] Install shade protection if needed
- [ ] Start regular feeding regime
- [ ] Monitor for pests daily

### Week 6: Establishment
- [ ] Train tomatoes up supports
- [ ] Remove first side shoots from tomatoes
- [ ] Feed flowering plants weekly
- [ ] Ensure consistent watering
- [ ] Check plants for signs of deficiency

### Week 7: Maintenance
- [ ] First harvest of quick crops (salad leaves, herbs)
- [ ] Continue side shooting and training
- [ ] Increase feeding as fruits form
- [ ] Second sowings of basil
- [ ] Enjoy your first greenhouse harvest!

### Week 8: Production
- [ ] Tomatoes setting fruit
- [ ] Peppers flowering
- [ ] Cucumbers climbing rapidly
- [ ] Daily watering routine established
- [ ] Regular harvesting begins

## 🚫 Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Consequence | Solution |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Sowing too early** | Leggy, pot-bound plants | Stick to schedule |
| **Poor ventilation** | Fungal diseases | Open vents daily |
| **Irregular watering** | Blossom end rot, split fruits | Water consistently |
| **Over-feeding** | Leafy growth, no fruit | Feed only when flowering |
| **Crowding plants** | Poor air circulation, disease | Follow spacing guides |
| **Ignoring pests** | Infestations | Check plants daily |
| **No shade** | Scorched plants | Apply shading in May |

## 🌦 When Things Go Wrong

### Problem: Leggy Seedlings

**Causes:** Too warm, not enough light
**Solution:** Move to cooler, brighter spot; pot deeper; start again if severe

### Problem: Yellow Leaves

**Causes:** Over-watering, under-feeding, cold
**Solution:** Check drainage, feed weekly, increase temperature

### Problem: Flowers But No Fruit

**Causes:** Poor pollination, temperature stress, irregular watering
**Solution:** Hand pollinate, ensure consistent temps and watering

### Problem: Whitefly

**Causes:** Brought in on plants, poor ventilation
**Solution:** Sticky traps, Encarsia formosa (biological control), avoid chemicals

### Problem: Blossom End Rot

**Causes:** Irregular watering, calcium deficiency
**Solution:** Water consistently, add calcium if needed (crushed eggshells in planting hole)

## 💡 Pro Tips from Experienced Growers

1. **"The greenhouse is only as good as its ventilation"** - Open vents every day above 10°C
2. **"Feed the soil, not the plant"** - Add compost to borders annually
3. **"Grow what you eat"** - Don't waste space on crops you won't use
4. **"Succession sow herbs"** - Basil every 3 weeks for continuous supply
5. **"Clean in autumn, clean in spring"** - Hygiene prevents more problems than cures
6. **"Shade in summer, light in winter"** - Remove shading by September
7. **"Water in the morning"** - Plants dry before night, less fungal issues
