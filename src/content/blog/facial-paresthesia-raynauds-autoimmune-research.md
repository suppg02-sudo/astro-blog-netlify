# Facial Paresthesia, Raynaud s and Autoimmune Research

## My Symptoms

- Facial paresthesia with numbness in nose, cheeks, eyes, scalp, ears
- Numbness and tingling spreads radially from bridge of nose across to eyebrows, eyes, scalp, full nose, ears, lips
- Pain in right eye with swelling
- Skin issues including thickening, discoloration, and rash
- Topical stinging soreness
- Bags under eyes (periorbital edema)
- Slight numbness in fingers and toes
- Coexisting Raynaud s phenomenon
- Symptoms worse with cold exposure
- Left side more affected than right
- Chronic, gradual onset over extended period

## Research Findings

### Dermatome Mapping

The radial spread pattern is clinically significant because it follows specific nerve distributions:

1. Bridge of nose → V1 (ophthalmic) - nasociliary (first)
2. Eyebrows/eyes → V1 - supraorbital (second)
3. Scalp → V1 + cervical overlap (third)
4. Full nose → V1 + V2 (fourth)
5. Ears → V3/Greater auricular (fifth)
6. Lips → V2/V3 (sixth)

### Condition Confidence Scores

Age-adjusted (54), with radial spread pattern factored:

| Condition | Confidence | Reasoning |
|-----------|------------|------------|
| **Trigeminal Neuropathy** | **70%** | Classic V1→V2→V3 progression. Demyelinating or inflammatory cause possible |
| **Mixed Connective Tissue Disease** | **50%** | Raynaud s + neuropathy + paresthesia + cold sensitivity |
| **Multiple Sclerosis** | **55%** | Demyelinating plaques on trigeminal pathways |
| **Peripheral Neuropathy** | **50%** | Facial numbness + finger/toe numbness + chronic |
| **Lupus (SLE)** | **35%** | Periorbital edema possible, skin involvement, but classic malar rash absent |
| **Sjogren s Syndrome** | **30%** | Dry eye possible, periorbital edema, facial numbness |
| **Thyroid Dysfunction** | **25%** | Periorbital edema, skin changes, Raynaud s |
| **Systemic Sclerosis** | **20%** | Raynaud s fits but key skin thickening marker absent |
| **Vitamin B12 Deficiency** | **15%** | Peripheral neuropathy but bilateral usually |

### Recommended Tests (Priority Order)

| Test | What it checks | Priority |
|------|----------------|----------|
| **Neurology Referral** | Essential for trigeminal neuropathy pattern | Highest |
| **MRI Brain with Contrast** | Demyelinating lesions, trigeminal compression | Highest |
| **CBC + B12 + Folate** | Neuropathy causes | High |
| **ANA + ENA Panel** | Autoimmune screening | High |
| **Thyroid Panel (TSH, T3, T4)** | Thyroid dysfunction | Medium |
| **ESR/CRP** | Systemic inflammation | Medium |
| **Nerve Conduction Study** | Peripheral neuropathy assessment | Medium |
| **Lumbar Puncture** (if neurology recommends) | MS markers, oligoclonal bands | Conditional |

### Key Insight

The radial dermatomal progression following the trigeminal nerve anatomy is a strong indicator of neurological involvement, which could be due to compression, inflammation, or demyelination. This significantly changes the diagnostic picture toward a neurological cause rather than purely autoimmune.
