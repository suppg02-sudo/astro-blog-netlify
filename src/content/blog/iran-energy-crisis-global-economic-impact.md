# Iran Conflict Triggers Global Energy Crisis: 20% of World Gas Offline

The recent escalation in the Iran-Israel conflict has triggered what analysts are calling a "point of no return" for global energy markets. This analysis examines the cascading economic impacts already rippling through world economies, with actionable preparation guidance.

## Key Energy Infrastructure Impacts

### South Pars Natural Gas Field

- **World's largest** natural gas field, shared between Iran and Qatar
- **20% of global natural gas supply** now offline
- Conservative estimates suggest **5-7 years** before restoration
- Two of Qatar's 14 LNG trains completely destroyed in recent strikes

### Oil Supply Disruption

- **10% of global oil production** currently offline
- No immediate pathway to restore supply
- Simple supply-demand economics driving price increases

### Strait of Hormuz Factor

- Critical shipping chokepoint threatened
- Any closure would compound existing shortages exponentially

## Immediate Economic Consequences

### Energy Prices

- UK gas prices jumped **20% in a single trading session**
- Oil prices exceeding $100/barrel with upward trajectory
- LNG contract cancellations affecting Belgium, Italy, China, and South Korea

### Employment Impact

- **300,000+ British workers** at risk of redundancy in 2026
- This figure is **45% higher** than pandemic-era layoffs
- Employment scarring effect creating vicious economic cycle

### Broader Economic Indicators

- Global inflation projected to reach **3-4.5%**
- GDP contractions varying by country energy independence
- Stock markets positioned for decline
- Government bonds facing upward pressure (higher interest rates)

## Regional Impact Analysis

### United Kingdom

- Energy price cap expiring in July - severe shocks expected
- Bank of England considering rate increases (9-1 vote)
- Dual threats: Iran (for UK bases supporting US strikes) and Russia (for Ukraine support)
- Manufacturing and consumer cyclical sectors most vulnerable

### European Union

- EU leadership criticized as disconnected from citizen impact
- Energy-dependent economies facing severe contractions
- Austerity measures likely as government bond yields rise

### United States

- Better positioned due to domestic energy production
- Canadian energy imports providing buffer
- Gas pump prices still rising despite advantages

### Australia and New Zealand

- Predicted to be "hit really hard"
- Energy policy failures blamed on leadership
- Geographic isolation compounding supply challenges

## Cascading Effects Explained

### The Employment Scarring Cycle

1. Higher energy costs → Business margin compression
2. Business responses → Layoffs (employees are highest expense)
3. Laid-off workers → Reduced consumer spending
4. Reduced spending → Further business failures
5. Cycle repeats and accelerates

### Financial Market Implications

- **Gold and Silver**: Currently declining due to liquidation, expected to surge upward
- **Stock Markets**: Declining due to higher input costs and consumer cutbacks
- **Government Bonds**: Rising yields = less government spending capacity = austerity measures
- **Mortgages**: Potential return to 15-20% interest rates (comparable to 1970s-80s)

- **Fix mortgage rates now** if renewal within 1-2 years
