# The Iran Economic Shock: Why Resource Ownership Is Your Only Real Protection

> **TL;DR**: The US-Israel invasion of Iran and the blocking of the Strait of Hormuz will trigger massive energy and food price inflation. Government price caps will repeat COVID-era mistakes — transferring wealth from the public purse to the richest. The only real protection is ownership of resources, and the only way to reclaim ownership is through taxation.

*Based on [Gary's Economics — "The Iran economic shock is coming. How to protect yourself"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oi265I48MdI)*

## Quick Summary

- The Strait of Hormuz blockade will cause sustained energy and food price inflation worldwide
- Central banks may reverse rate-cutting cycles — UK markets expect 2-3 hikes back to ~4.5%
- Government energy subsidies would repeat COVID mistakes: drain public wealth, enrich the richest
- Domestic energy production doesn't protect you if you don't **own** it — look at the US
- The only real protection: ownership of resources (housing, energy, food)
- The only path to reclaim ownership: a tax system that recaptures wealth from the richest

## What's Happening

The US and Israel have invaded Iran. In response, Iran has blocked the Strait of Hormuz — the narrow waterway through which an enormous share of global oil and gas flows. The duration of the blockade is unknown (days to months), but the economic consequences are already locked in.

**Three immediate impacts:**

1. **Energy prices surge** — oil and gas costs rising sharply
2. **Food prices follow** — fertiliser and transport costs push food inflation higher
3. **Interest rate reversal** — central banks worldwide were cutting rates; markets now expect rate hikes to combat inflation

## The Interest Rate Shock Nobody's Talking About

The UK base rate sits at 3.75%. Financial markets now expect two or possibly three hikes this year, pushing the base rate back to 4.5%. This means:

- **Mortgage rates will rise** — anyone remortgaging faces higher costs
- **Government borrowing costs surge** — UK 10-year gilt yields have jumped from ~4.2% to nearly 5% in a month
- **Fiscal space evaporates** — the government's ability to support citizens during this crisis is severely constrained

The UK already has the highest borrowing costs in Europe. Combined with low growth and high debt, the government may struggle to respond — and may even need further austerity.

## Why Government Price Caps Are a Trap

The obvious political response — capping energy prices — is economically dangerous. Here's why:

### The COVID Playbook Repeated

During COVID, governments borrowed and printed enormous sums to pay people's wages. The result:

- Government wealth collapsed
- The richest accumulated massive wealth
- Inequality surged
- Austerity followed

An energy price cap works the same way: the government pays the difference between market price and capped price. That money flows directly to energy owners — who are overwhelmingly the wealthiest people in society.

### Three Fatal Flaws of Price Caps

1. **You still pay** — through taxation or loss of government wealth, you bear the cost
2. **No conservation incentive** — if prices stay artificially low, nobody reduces consumption, making the subsidy even more expensive
3. **Regressive outcome** — rich people don't change their energy usage regardless of price; poor people still end up cutting back

### A Sensible Alternative

A rational government would focus on reducing the **most wasteful** energy usage — private jets, excessive consumption — rather than forcing the poor to use less. But this requires confronting wealth and power.

## Why Domestic Energy Production Won't Save You

The US is the world's largest oil producer thanks to decades of fracking investment. Are American citizens protected from energy price shocks? No.

**Because they don't own the oil.**

Those energy facilities are owned by incredibly rich people who will sell oil abroad to other rich people. Having energy in your country's backyard means nothing if you don't have ownership of it.

## The Real Answer: Ownership of Resources

Gary Stevenson (former Citibank trader and author of *The Game of Wealth*) came into this crisis owning hundreds of thousands of pounds worth of oil — not because he's a genius commodities trader, but **because he's rich**.

Like most wealthy people, he owns a broadly diversified portfolio: property, stocks, bonds, oil, wheat futures, cotton futures. When oil prices surged 50%, he made hundreds of thousands automatically.

> *"If you are watching from America and you are paying more for energy despite the fact that there is a ton of oil in your country, that's because you don't own your oil. I do."*

**This is what wealth distribution actually means:**

| Resource | How to Protect Yourself |
|----------|------------------------|
| Housing | Own your home — rents will keep rising |
| Energy | Own energy assets — they rise when prices surge |
| Food | Own agricultural commodities — they track food inflation |
| General | Own a diversified asset portfolio |

## 50 Years of Asset Drain

This wasn't always the case. In the post-war period, Western governments understood that citizens needed to own resources:

- **Governments owned housing** — council housing guaranteed shelter
- **Governments owned energy, transport, water** — public utilities provided security
- **Working families accumulated assets** — a postman on £20K could buy a house

All of that has been systematically stripped away. Government wealth has collapsed. Working families have lost their assets. The middle class is losing its assets. The flow is consistently in one direction: away from ordinary people, toward the ultra-wealthy.

Every crisis — 2008, 2011 sovereign debt, COVID, Ukraine, now Iran — accelerates this transfer.

## The Only Way Back: Tax

There is no mechanism to reverse this asset drain other than taxation. Not because it's ideologically satisfying, but because it's **the only lever available**:

> *"How the hell else do you get your resources back?"*

A tax system capable of taxing the richest people at fair rates is the only way to:
- Rebuild government wealth (and thus government capacity to protect citizens)
- Give working families a meaningful share of national resources
- Break the cycle where every crisis makes the rich richer and everyone else poorer

## The Deeper Warning: Inequality and War

Stevenson raises a chilling connection between extreme inequality and armed conflict. His Oxford thesis demonstrated that high inequality produces high asset prices and low wages — making people cheap and assets expensive.

When people are cheap and assets are expensive, war becomes economically rational for the powerful:

- **People are cheap** — soldiers cost little
- **Assets are expensive** — seizing them is enormously profitable
- **Power is concentrated** — a tiny elite commands military force

Historically, medieval elites spent their wealth primarily on armies to fight each other. Today's billionaire class commands similar power through state apparatus.

<summary>📖 Deep Dive: The Squeeze Mechanism</summary>

The wealth transfer operates in three stages:

**Stage 1 — Squeeze the working class**: Post-1960s, working-class families could accumulate assets. That's no longer possible. Wages have stagnated while asset prices have soared.

**Stage 2 — Squeeze the government**: Western governments once owned substantial wealth (housing, utilities, infrastructure). Decades of privatisation and crisis-driven borrowing have drained this.

**Stage 3 — Squeeze the middle class**: Now even middle-class and upper-middle-class families can't buy housing. The squeeze moves progressively up the income ladder.

Each crisis accelerates the transfer. The Iran energy shock will be no different unless citizens demand structural change through taxation.
