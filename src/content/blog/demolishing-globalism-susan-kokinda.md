### What the American System Actually Is

- **Economics grounded in real physical production**: not about money accumulation, but about whether society produces what it needs to survive and grow
- **Credit, tax, and banking policies** shaped to create conditions for productive growth — not to maximize returns for financial intermediaries
- **Tariffs as industrial policy**: protection for domestic producers until they can compete globally
- **National banking** (distinct from central banking): credit directed at productive investment, not speculation

Kokinda notes that the system was "baked into" America's mid-20th century prosperity — the era when one blue-collar income could comfortably raise a family. That was dismantled deliberately over roughly 50 years, with NAFTA and the 2008 financial crisis as the two most visible inflection points.

## Trump's Policies as American System in Action

Kokinda's core claim: what looks like a disjointed series of Trump policies is actually a coherent, systematic dismantling of the globalist framework.

### Tariffs

The most visible pillar. The counter-narrative to "tariffs cause inflation":

> "It's not going to make it more expensive if you build it here. You bring it back home, you build it here, you give somebody a good-paying job, and you're not going to pay tariffs. It's called win-win as opposed to lose-lose."

Trade Ambassador Jameson Greer [explicitly named the American System](https://ustr.gov) in a speech at Davos — a historic moment, since the framework had been absent from official US economic discourse for decades.

### The $17 Trillion Manufacturing Reinvestment

Foreign investment flowing back into US factories represents the physical manifestation of the policy shift — creating the blue-collar jobs that form the backbone of the American System's social contract.

### Federal Reserve and Financial Policy

Both Trump's Fed pick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessant have signaled a clean break from the post-2008 orthodoxy of *stabilizing big financial institutions at all costs*:

> "The primary purpose of the economic policy of the United States should be the productive economy and household incomes."

Kokinda calls this "pretty damn revolutionary" — explicitly telling JP Morgan and Citibank they're no longer first in line.

### Project Vault: Critical Minerals Bank

One of the less-discussed but most important initiatives:
- Uses the Export-Import Bank to provide government credit backing for domestic mining and minerals processing
- **Price protection**: shields domestic producers from Chinese undercutting strategies
- Addresses the dependency created when China controls rare earth supply chains
- A textbook application of the American System's credit and industrial policy principles

### Taking On "The Bigs"

Simultaneously, Trump is challenging cartelized sectors that previous administrations — including populist ones — left untouched:

| **Big Pharma** | Most Favored Nations pricing to drive drug costs down |

## Election Integrity: The Achilles Heel

If there is one vulnerability in the reform agenda, Kokinda agrees it is **election integrity**. The logic:

- You can have an informed, motivated citizenry
- But if elections can be manipulated, the republic's self-correction mechanism is broken
- The enemy's most efficient bottleneck is controlling the electoral process

### How the Fraud Infrastructure Actually Works

It's not primarily the political parties running fraud — it's **501(c)(3) nonprofits and foundations**:
- Profile populations and create voter databases
- Identify precincts where turnout will be low
- Ensure ballots appear in those precincts under absent voters' names

The Trump administration is quietly investigating these organizations. Kokinda notes this won't generate big headlines, but could dry up the infrastructure that enables fraud.

### The Save Act

Simple, achievable: require proof of citizenship to vote. Polling shows 80%+ support across all demographics. The obvious reform that opponents have twisted into a civil rights issue despite universal common-sense appeal.

### The 2026 Strategy: Make It Too Big to Rig Again

The 2024 model — which worked:
- Recruit voters the Democrats don't expect
- Overperform in unexpected demographics and geographies
- Create a margin so large that fraud cannot plausibly cover it

In Michigan: the Dearborn/Hamtramck Muslim American vote shifting toward Trump, combined with Macomb County auto worker margins and small but meaningful Black voter gains in Detroit, made Wayne County's expected fraud margin mathematically impossible to deploy.

## The 5% vs. 5% Principle

One of the most clarifying frameworks from the conversation:

> "It's not 5% against 95%. It's 5% versus 5% and 90% of people that are either too stupid or lack the backbone or just don't care."

**Implication**: The battle is not to convince a hostile majority. It's to shift what *appears* to be the consensus, and the 90% will flow toward whatever looks like the winning side.

**Evidence**: People who furiously opposed heterodox COVID coverage in 2020 now quietly say "yeah, there were some issues with mRNA" — without ever admitting they were wrong. The goal isn't "I told you so"; it's normalization.

Prediction: In five years, manufacturing-first, America-First economics will be the boring, mainstream take. Not radical. Like air travel becoming a mundane commute.
