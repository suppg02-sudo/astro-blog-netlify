# Inside the Iran-US Crisis: What the Markets Are Dangerously Ignoring

The markets are celebrating. The headlines are optimistic. But according to geopolitical strategist Simon Hunt, we're walking straight into a trap of false peace—one that could end with gold skyrocketing to $38,000 and a global war that reshapes the world order for decades.

In a riveting interview on CapitalCosm, dated April 14, 2026—just two days after the Islamabad negotiations between Iran and the United States, and one day into the US Navy blockade of the Strait of Hormuz—Hunt laid out a chilling assessment that contradicts almost everything the mainstream financial media is telling you.

The bulls are charging. The VIX is sleeping. But the smart money? They're loading up on gold and preparing for escalation.

## The Islamabad Illusion: What Really Happened Behind Closed Doors

Here's what the public knows: Iran and the US sat down in Islamabad for negotiations. A ceasefire was announced. Everyone went home happy.

Here's what actually happened, according to Hunt's sources within the Iranian delegation:

J.D. Vance was on the phone constantly to Washington. At several moments, the two sides were tantalizingly close to a deal—only to have it squashed immediately after Vance's calls back to the White House.

The most revealing detail? Trump reportedly wanted to "share the spoils" of the Strait of Hormuz with Iran. Yes, you read that correctly. The former president proposed a joint control arrangement over one of the world's most critical energy chokepoints—a proposal Iran would never accept in a million years.

Then, the discussions ended abruptly. The Iranian delegation wasn't even informed. Vance boarded his plane back to Washington, leaving Kushner and Witkoff reportedly staying behind.

Does that sound like a successful negotiation to you?

## The Real Reason Both Sides Wanted a "Ceasefire"

Hunt cuts through the noise with surgical precision: **both sides wanted a pause to rearm and reposition.**

For Iran, the calculus is straightforward. Just days before the Islamabad talks, China flew in three massive cargo planes loaded with sophisticated military equipment. Iran needed time to install and integrate these systems before the next round of hostilities.

For the United States, the situation is equally transparent. There are reports of massive US troop movements into the region—tens of thousands of soldiers, fleets of aircraft, and naval assets being positioned for what comes next.

This isn't cheap. This isn't posturing. You don't move an armada into position if you're planning for peace.

Hunt notes that the gap between Iran's 10-point plan and Washington's 15-point plan is simply too vast to bridge. The latest rumor—that Iran offered to cease uranium production for five years while Trump demanded twenty—further confirms that neither side is negotiating in good faith. They're buying time.

## The Bigger Game: Energy Dominance and the Dismantling of BRICS

Why is Trump so obsessed with Iran? It's not just about supporting Israel. Hunt identifies two strategic objectives that explain everything:

**First: Energy Supremacy.** Having already secured Venezuela and expanded America's presence in Nigeria, Trump wants control of Iran's massive energy reserves. If he achieves this, he effectively controls the entire Persian Gulf—and by extension, a devastating amount of the world's oil supply.

**Second: Dismantling BRICS.** Iran serves as a pivotal logistical hub for the BRICS alliance (Brazil, Russia, India, China, South Africa). If America neutralizes Iran, it doesn't just remove a geopolitical adversary—it fractures the entire multipolar coalition that threatens American unipolar dominance.

This isn't a regional conflict. This is a proxy war for the future of the global order.

## China's Calculated Moves and the Railroad Bombing

Perhaps the most telling detail Hunt reveals is what happened to China's infrastructure investments in Iran. During the recent hostilities, America and Israel bombed a massive railway that China had built and financed—a critical artery of the north-south corridor that Iran uses to transport oil and cargo to its eastern ally.

Let that sink in. The United States and Israel directly destroyed Chinese infrastructure. In any other era, that would be considered an act of war against China itself.

Yet the world barely noticed.

China's response? Flying in advanced military equipment and quietly positioning itself for the larger confrontation ahead. They're playing the long game—something Hunt suggests will culminate in a massive geopolitical "blowoff" between 2028 and 2030 that will determine whether we live in an American-led unipolar world or a multipolar world led by China, Russia, and Iran.

## Where Does Iran Attack Next? Hunt's Prediction

Hunt doesn't deal in vague generalities. He believes the next major American-Israeli attack will target **Bandar Abbas**, Iran's critical port city—not Kharg Island or other locations that have been speculated in the press.

If Hunt knows this, Iran certainly knows this. Which means they're currently repositioning their defenses accordingly. The chess pieces are moving, even if the cameras have turned away.

Amid all this geopolitical analysis, Hunt drops a number that should make every investor sit up straight: **$38,000 gold.**

Not this year. Not next year. But by the 2030-2032 period, as the culmination of these geopolitical shocks ripples through the global financial system.

The markets are pricing in peace. Simon Hunt is pricing in war.
