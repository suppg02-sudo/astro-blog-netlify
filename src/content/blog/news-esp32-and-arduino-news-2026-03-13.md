### New Boards & AI Integration

**Arduino Unveils VENTUNO Q for Edge AI and Robotics**
Arduino has announced the upcoming launch of its revolutionary Arduino VENTUNO Q platform, purpose-built for generative AI, robotics, and actuation. Revealed on March 9, 2026, ahead of Embedded World, this board features a Qualcomm Dragonwing IQ8 Series processor, delivering up to 40 TOPS of NPU acceleration for AI workloads, coupled with a dedicated STM32H5 microcontroller for deterministic real-time control. The VENTUNO Q aims to unify perception, decision, and action on a single board, supporting local LLMs, VLMs, and integration with Edge Impulse Studio through the Arduino App Lab environment.

**Arduino and Qualcomm Launch Hackster.io Edge AI Contest**
In a move to foster community engagement and innovation, Arduino and Qualcomm Technologies, Inc. kicked off a global developer contest on Hackster.io on March 3, 2026. The competition invites engineers and makers to create groundbreaking projects using the Arduino UNO Q platform, which combines a Qualcomm Dragonwing QRB2210 MPU with an STM32 microcontroller. Organizers are giving away 300 Arduino UNO Q boards to support participants in accelerating innovation in AI, IoT, and robotics.

**2026 ESP32 Selection Guides Highlight Evolving Chip Landscape**
Several comprehensive guides released in early March 2026 are helping developers navigate the expanding ESP32 family. **ESP32 Transforms into Thermal USB Webcam and Pulse Metal Detector**
The maker community continues to push the boundaries of ESP32 applications, as seen in recent projects featured in early March 2026. One innovator transformed an ESP32-based board with a 32x24 IR sensor into a thermal USB webcam, upscaling to 320x240 and streaming MJPEG. **ESP32 Powers Efficient Bluetooth Proxy for Home Assistant**
Developers are leveraging the ESP32's dual-core capabilities to create efficient Bluetooth Proxies for Home Assistant. An ESP32 can simultaneously handle Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, offloading processing from a central server and providing a dedicated, low-power interface for sensors. This allows existing ESP32-based devices to integrate seamlessly into smart home ecosystems by simply adding Bluetooth Proxy code to their configuration.
