## Part 1: The Divine Name YHWH

### Wisdom of Solomon: The Tetragrammaton

The Book of Wisdom uses the Hebrew tetragrammaton directly:

> "O judges of the land, love justice! Think well of **YHWH**, and seek Him with purity of heart. For He will be found by those who have not tested him, reveal himself to those who believe in Him. And malicious thoughts separate from **YHWH**, and on the day of vengeance will the fools' foolishness become known."

Key themes:
- YHWH rewards the faithful
- YHWH judges the wicked
- Wisdom comes through seeking YHWH

## Part 2: JAH in Meqabyan

The Meqabyan books use "JAH" (Iyaric transliteration) extensively:

### JAH Saves David

> "An the 'Amalieqans arose - pon the time when the one from the four Rafayn arose - **JAH** saved Daweet from all this tribulation that enemies who disputed him brought pon him. As prevailin are by **JAH** Accord - them were defeated by them enemies hand - yet but **JAH** didn't save the evil kings who didn't believe ina Him."

### JAH's Faithfulness

> "An Hiziqyas believed ina **JAH** ~ Him saved him from Senakriem hand who were arrogant. But him child Minassie were defeated by him enemy hand - fe him didn't mek him trustin ina **JAH**."

### The Cycle of Repentance

> "An them will return toward sin again that them might sadden Him - an Him will arouse them enemies peoples ina them area pon them ~ them will downstroy them an kill them an capture them. An again them will shout toward **JAH** ina mournin an sadness - an there are the time when Him sent help an saved them by prophets hands."

## Part 3: The Idols

### King Tseerutsaydan's Idol System

7 Meqabyan 1 describes an elaborate idol worship system:

#### The Priests

> "Him appointed priests who serve him idols. While them ate from that defouled sacrifice - them would tell him pretendin that the idols eat night an day. Again them would mek other persons diligent like unto them - that them might sacrifice sacrifice an eat."

#### The Idols

> "But that Tseerutsaydan were arrogant ~ him had **fifty idols worked ina males pattern an twenty worked ina dawtaz pattern**. An him would boast ina those idols that have no benefit ~ him would totally glorify them while him sacrificed sacrifice mornin an evenin."

#### Daily Sacrifices

> "Him appointed keepers fe them there ~ him would Itinually sacrifice forty fe him idols - ten fattened oxen - ten sterile cows - ten fattened sheep ewes - ten barren goats - with birds that have wings."

---

### Bi'iel Fiegor: The Idol Examined

7 Meqabyan 2 provides a devastating critique of idol worship:

> "An again them would worship a idol called **Bi'iel Fiegor** ~ them would trust it like unto them Irator JAH when it were deaf an dumb. Fe it are the idol that a person hand worked - fe it are the person hand Work that a smith worked who work silver an gold - that have no breath nor knowledge."

The text catalogs the idol's impotence:
- Cannot eat or drink
- Cannot kill or save
- Cannot plant or uproot
- Cannot harm enemies or benefit friends
- Has no breath, soul, or knowledge

---

### Dagon: The Philistine Idol

3 Tobit references Dagon during the story of Sennacherib:

> "And the saying came to Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons, and they laid in wait for him, and killed him with the sword, at the hour when he went in to pray before his idol **Dagon**, as it is said, 'And Adrammelech and Sharezer his sons smote him with the sword, and they escaped into the land of Ararat.'"

Sennacherib was killed by his own sons while praying to Dagon.

## Part 5: The Martyrs' Testimony

Those who refused to worship idols spoke boldly:

> "As fe InI - InI won't bow nor sacrifice fe defouled idols that have no knowledge nor reasonin. InI won't bow fe idols that were silver an gold that a person hand worked - that were stone an wood - that have no reasonin nor soul nor knowledge - that don't benefit them friends nor harm them enemies."

The martyrs recognized:
- Idols are made by human hands
- They have no knowledge or reason
- They cannot benefit friends or harm enemies
- They are merely stone, wood, silver, and gold

## Conclusion

The Ethiopian texts present a clear contrast:
- **YHWH/JAH**: The living God who saves, judges, and responds to faith
- **Idols**: Dead objects made by human hands, unable to act or respond

The Meqabyan martyrs chose death rather than worship idols, testifying that YHWH alone is God.
