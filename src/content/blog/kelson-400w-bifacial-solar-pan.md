## Why These Panels Stand Out

If you've ever tried to buy solar panels online in small quantities, you know the struggle. They're expensive to ship, hard to find in manageable sizes, and the specs often don't play nice with portable power stations. The Kelson 200W bifacial panels solve all three problems.

### The Voltage Sweet Spot

The real magic is in the voltage. With a Voc (open circuit voltage) of 27.3V, you can wire two panels in series for roughly 44V — well within the 60V max input that dominates the portable power station market. This gives you:

- Room to add more panels in parallel without hitting voltage limits
- Better amperage delivery compared to higher-voltage panels at the same wattage
- Compatibility with the EcoFlow Delta 2, Delta 2 Max, Delta 1300, Anker F2000, Bluetti Apex 300, and most AllPower units

Compare that to older 200W panels with a Voc of ~22.5V — wire two of those in series and you're already at 45V, with less headroom for expanding your array.

### Cell Technology: Half-Cut with 16 Bus Bars

These panels use half-cut cells with 16 bus bars — a significant upgrade from older panels that use fewer bus bars and continuous full-length cells. The result:

- **10W more output in full sun** compared to same-rated older panels (163W vs 155W in testing)
- More efficient electron pathways mean less resistance and better heat management

## Real-World Shade Testing

This is where the split-cell design really shines. The panel is effectively two halves separated by a silver divider:

| Shade Scenario | Old Panel | Kelson Panel |
|---|---|---|
| Full sun | 155W | 163W |
| Bottom corner shaded | 87W (44% drop) | 90W (45% drop) |
| Full bottom shaded | 0W (total loss) | 84W (still producing) |
| Full top shaded | N/A | 86W |
| Middle bridge (across split) | 65W | 0W |

The key takeaway: when shade hits one half of the Kelson panel, the other half continues producing at near-full capacity. On the old panel, shade anywhere pulls the entire panel down. The only scenario where the old panel wins is when shade bridges the split point — but that's rare in real-world conditions.

Over time, the Kelson's ability to keep producing through partial shade will net you more total energy harvested.

## Standalone MPPT Compatibility

For those running standalone solar charge controllers, these panels are equally impressive. A popular 40A MPPT controller accepting up to 150V can handle five panels in series (5 x 27.3V = 136.5V) with cold-morning headroom to spare. Pair that with a 48V battery bank and you're pushing 2,000W of solar through a single controller.
