## What is OSINT?

Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) is intelligence that's available for average citizens online. It's information that's publicly accessible - sometimes free, sometimes paid access - but you don't need to be part of a three-letter agency to access it. It's just stuff that's out there.

This guide focuses on **free tools** for OSINT investigations.

## OSINT Tool Categories

OSINT tools can be sorted into four main categories (with some overlap between them):

### 1. Search Tools
General search engines and specialized search platforms for finding information.

### 2. Geographic/Mapping Tools
Satellite imagery, mapping services, and geolocation tools.

### 3. Raw Data Tools
Public datasets, databases, and raw information sources available online.

### 4. Social Media/Human Intelligence (HUMINT)
**Note**: You'd be surprised how much OSINT information comes from social media. Even conflict trackers always link to sources, which are usually X (Twitter) or Telegram. I wouldn't be surprised if 90% of OSINT intelligence comes from social media platforms.

**Pitfalls**: Human intelligence needs to be analyzed very closely. You must understand psychology and ask critical questions: who, what, where, when, why. Are you falling for deception (psychological operations), or is there real actionable intelligence in what they're conveying?

## OSINT Investigation Philosophy

Running an open-source investigation requires:

- **Critical thinking**: Not everything you find is accurate
- **Source verification**: Always cross-reference multiple sources
- **Understanding biases**: Every source has an agenda
- **Psychological awareness**: Recognize disinformation and manipulation
- **Technical skills**: Using the right tools efficiently

## Key OSINT Principles

1. **Start broad, then narrow down**
2. **Verify everything** - especially social media claims
3. **Document your process** for reproducibility
4. **Protect yourself** - don't reveal your investigation methods
5. **Stay ethical** - use information responsibly
